Questions

Ask me anything.

The things people ask most, plus what the words on your quote actually mean.

Do you charge for quotes?

Quotes for planned work such as bathrooms, showers and pipework upgrades are free and written down, with labour, materials and any extras listed separately. For emergencies and small repairs we give you our call-out rate on the phone before we set off, so you know the cost before we arrive.

Are you insured?

Any plumber working in your home should carry public liability insurance. Ask us for our insurance details before work starts and we will show you the certificate. It is a sensible question to ask any trade, and a good firm will not mind.

Do you do gas work?

Plumbing and gas are separate skills with separate legal requirements. Any work on gas appliances or gas pipework must be done by a registered gas engineer, and it is illegal for anyone else to do it. If your job involves gas, ask to see the engineer's gas engineer ID card, which lists the types of work they are qualified for.

How do you take payment?

Small jobs are paid on completion by bank transfer or card. Larger jobs such as bathroom refits usually have a deposit to cover materials and a final payment when the work is finished and you are happy. The payment schedule is written on your quote, so there are no surprises.

What guarantee do I get?

Our workmanship is guaranteed, and the length of that guarantee is written on your quote. Products carry their own manufacturer's guarantees, and we will pass on the paperwork and registration details. If something we fitted fails, call us and we will come back.

How messy will it be?

We use dust sheets and floor protection, keep the work area tidy as we go, and take away old fittings and packaging. Bathroom strip-outs are dusty, so we seal doorways where we can. We leave the room clean enough to use, not a building site.

Can you come out in an emergency?

We prioritise genuine emergencies such as burst pipes, uncontrollable leaks and no water. Call us and we will tell you honestly how soon we can get to you. In the meantime, turn off the water at the stopcock and, if water is near electrics, switch off the power.

Do I need to be home while you work?

For the survey and the first morning of a bigger job, yes, so we can agree the details. After that, many customers leave us a key and go to work. We will keep you updated with photos and messages, and let you know if anything unexpected turns up.

Jargon buster

Words on your quote.

Stopcock
The main valve that shuts off the water supply to the whole house, usually under the kitchen sink.
Isolation valve
A small valve on the pipe to one tap or toilet, so it can be turned off without shutting off the whole house.
Combi boiler
A boiler that heats water on demand straight from the mains, with no hot water cylinder or loft tanks.
Gravity system
Hot and cold water fed by tanks in the loft, where the height of the tank sets the water pressure.
Unvented cylinder
A sealed hot water cylinder fed directly from the mains, giving mains-pressure hot water to every tap.
Flow rate
How many litres per minute reach a tap or shower. Often more important than pressure for how a shower feels.
Trap
The U-shaped bend under a sink or bath that holds water to stop drain smells coming back up.
Soil pipe
The large 110mm pipe that carries toilet waste to the drains.
Double check valve
A valve that stops water flowing backwards into the drinking supply, required on outside taps.
Push-fit
Pipe fittings that join by pushing the pipe in, with no soldering. Quick, but best kept where they can be reached.
Macerator
A pump that grinds and pumps toilet waste through a small pipe, used where a normal soil pipe cannot reach.
