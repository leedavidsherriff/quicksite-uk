New bathrooms, fitted properly from the pipes up

Complete bathroom and en-suite refits, from strip-out to the last bead of silicone.

A new bathroom is one of the few home jobs where the part you never see matters most. Tiles and taps are the easy bit; the pipe runs, waste falls, water pressure and waterproofing behind the walls decide whether it still looks good in ten years. We fit complete bathrooms and en-suites for homeowners and landlords, handling the strip-out, first-fix pipework, suite, shower and finishing as one job, so you are not left chasing three different trades. Whether it is a like-for-like refresh in a 1930s semi or a full change of layout, you get a written price and a clear order of works before anything comes off the wall.

Keeping the layout versus moving things around

The biggest single driver of cost is the toilet. Its 110mm soil pipe needs a steady fall to the stack, so moving it more than a metre or so often means lifting floorboards, boxing in, or fitting a macerator, which works but is noisier and needs occasional maintenance. Basins and baths are far easier to move because their 40mm wastes can run further. If you want a new layout, we check the joist direction and the route to the soil stack at the survey, then tell you honestly which moves are cheap and which ones add a day or two and several hundred pounds.

Water pressure decides your shower

Before you fall for a rainfall head, it is worth knowing what your system can feed. A combi boiler delivers mains-pressure hot water but only so many litres a minute, so a large drench head can feel weak if someone runs a tap. A gravity system with a tank in the loft usually needs a pump to run a mixer shower well. We test the flow rate and pressure at the survey and recommend valves and heads that suit what you have, or explain what an upgrade such as an unvented cylinder would involve.

Waterproofing and the stuff behind the tiles

Most failed bathrooms fail at the shower. Plasterboard behind a shower will soften if water gets through the grout, so wet zones should be lined with cement board or a dedicated tile backer board, with a tanking membrane and sealed corners where the walls meet the tray. Trays need a solid, level bed so they do not flex and crack the silicone. We also fit isolation valves on every supply, so a future tap or toilet repair does not mean draining the house, and leave access to traps where a wall panel allows.

Choosing a suite that lasts

Mid-range sanitaryware from established brands is usually the sweet spot: vitreous china that will not craze, soft-close seats, and spare parts you can still buy in five years. Cheap acrylic baths flex unless they are properly supported on a frame and cradle, so we always bed them properly. Wall-hung toilets and basins look clean and make floor cleaning easy, but need a proper support frame built into the wall. If you are buying your own suite, send us the product links before ordering and we will check fittings, sizes and lead times.

Questions about bathroom installation

How much does a new bathroom cost in the UK?

A typical family bathroom refit with a mid-range suite and tiling to the wet areas costs roughly £6,400 to £7,300 supply and fit. Moving the toilet, tiling floor to ceiling or choosing a wet room pushes that up. Your written quote breaks down labour, materials and any extras so you can see exactly where the money goes.

Can I buy my own bathroom suite?

Yes. Plenty of customers do. Send us the product links before you order and we will check the sizes, the tap holes, the waste types and whether the shower valve suits your water pressure. We cannot guarantee the items themselves, but we will guarantee our fitting, and we will tell you straight if something is likely to cause problems.

Will I be without a toilet during the work?

If it is your only toilet, we plan the job so you are without one for as short a time as possible, usually just part of a day while the new pan goes in. Where the layout allows, we keep the old toilet connected until the new pipework is ready. We agree the plan with you before we start.

Do I need planning permission or building regs for a new bathroom?

A like-for-like bathroom inside an existing house rarely needs planning permission. Building regulations can apply if you add new drainage, new electrical circuits or an extractor fan in a room that had none. Electrical work near the bath or shower is notifiable, so it must be done or certified by a registered electrician. We flag anything like this at the survey.
