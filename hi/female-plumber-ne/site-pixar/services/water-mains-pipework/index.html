Stopcocks, supply pipes and outside taps

New stopcocks, outside taps, lead pipe replacement and upgraded supply pipes.

Get my guide priceCall 07359 671340

The pipe bringing water into your home does more work than any other and gets the least attention. In older houses it may still be lead, or a narrow pipe that cannot feed a modern combi boiler and two showers at once. Stopcocks seize, outside taps freeze and split, and old pipe runs under floors corrode quietly. We replace stopcocks, fit outside taps with the right backflow protection, upgrade internal pipework and replace lead or undersized supply pipes. This page explains who owns which part of the pipe, and what an upgrade can do for your pressure.

Who owns which pipe

In most of the UK the water company is responsible for the pipe up to the boundary of your property, usually at the external stop tap. The supply pipe from there into your house is the homeowner's responsibility, as is everything inside. That matters if your pressure is poor or you find a leak on the drive, because the fix and the cost depend on which side of the boundary it sits. Your water company will often check pressure at the boundary for free, which tells us whether the problem is theirs or inside your property.

Lead pipes and poor pressure

Homes built before about 1970 may still have lead supply pipes, which can let lead leach into drinking water, particularly in soft water areas. Lead pipe is dull grey, soft, and does not ring when tapped. Replacing it with a modern blue polyethylene pipe removes the risk and, because the new pipe is usually wider, often improves flow noticeably. Some water companies run schemes that replace their side of the connection free or contribute when you replace yours, so we check this before quoting.

Stopcocks you can actually turn

An old brass stopcock that has not been turned in years often seizes solid or drips from the spindle when you finally try it. A modern quarter-turn lever valve is quick to operate in an emergency. We usually fit a new stopcock with a drain-off valve next to it, so the system can be emptied for future work without cutting pipes. If your stopcock is somewhere awkward, like behind a fitted kitchen unit, it can often be moved or a second valve added somewhere easy to reach.

Outside taps done right

An outside tap must have a double check valve to stop garden water, from a hose left in a bucket or a pond, being drawn back into the drinking supply. It also needs an isolation valve inside the house, so it can be drained before winter. Outside taps that freeze and split are one of the most common winter call-outs. Insulated covers help, but the best protection is turning off the inside valve and opening the tap to drain it before the first frost.

How long it takes

A few hours for a stopcock or outside tap; one to two days for a new supply pipe

Questions about water mains & pipework

How do I know if I have lead pipes?

Look at the pipe coming up through the floor to your internal stopcock. Lead is dull grey, soft enough to mark with a coin, and has rounded, bulging joints. If you scrape it gently it shows shiny silver underneath. Copper is orange-brown and modern plastic supply pipes are usually blue. If in doubt, we can check it for you.

Why is my water pressure low?

Common causes are an undersized or lead supply pipe, a partly closed or failing stopcock, corroded pipework, or low pressure in the water company's main. Your water company can test pressure at the boundary. If that is fine, the problem is inside your property and a flow test helps pinpoint it.

Do outside taps need a check valve?

Yes. The water regulations require outside taps to be protected against backflow, so garden water cannot be drawn back into the drinking supply. Most new outside tap kits include a double check valve. Older outside taps often do not have one and can be upgraded cheaply.
