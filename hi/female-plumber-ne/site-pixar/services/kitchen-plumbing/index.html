Kitchen sinks, taps and appliances plumbed in

Sinks, taps, washing machines, dishwashers and boiling-water taps fitted neatly.

Get my guide priceCall 07359 671340

Kitchen plumbing sits behind the cupboard doors, which is exactly why it is so often done badly: kinked hoses, wastes without traps, and connections nobody can reach. We plumb in new sinks and taps, washing machines, dishwashers, fridge freezers with water feeds and boiling-water taps, whether you are fitting a new kitchen or just replacing an appliance. We work with kitchen fitters or on our own. Everything is connected with proper valves and traps, tested under pressure and left so you can reach it if something ever needs attention.

Sinks and wastes that drain properly

A sink waste needs a trap to stop drain smells, and a steady fall to the outside gully or stack. Where a washing machine or dishwasher shares the waste, the connection should go into a proper appliance spigot on the trap or a standpipe, not a hole cut into the pipe. Long waste runs across the back of a kitchen need enough fall and support to stop them sagging and collecting grease. When a new kitchen moves the sink, we check the route to the drain before the units go in.

Washing machines and dishwashers

Each appliance needs its own cold supply with a service valve, fed from a proper tee on the pipework rather than a self-cutting valve clamped onto the pipe, which can leak over time. The drain hose needs a high loop or a standpipe to stop dirty water siphoning back. Integrated appliances also need enough space behind the unit for the hoses not to kink. If you are moving appliances to a utility room, we can run new supplies and wastes to the new position.

Boiling-water and filter taps

Boiling-water taps have a tank under the sink that heats water close to 100°C, plus a filter and often an expansion device. They need a cold supply, a nearby fused electrical spur and space in the sink unit. Many require a specific pressure range, and some need a pressure-reducing valve if your mains pressure is high. We check the manufacturer's requirements before fitting, because the guarantee on these taps often depends on correct installation. Filter cartridges need changing on a schedule, typically every six to twelve months.

Working alongside your kitchen fitter

On a new kitchen, plumbing happens twice. First fix runs supplies and wastes to the right positions before the units go in; second fix connects the sink, taps and appliances once the worktops are down. Getting the first fix positions right from the kitchen plan saves drilling through new cabinet backs later. We are happy to work from your kitchen designer's drawings and coordinate with your fitter so the job flows without anyone waiting around. We also check the sink cut-out and tap hole before the worktop is fitted.

How long it takes

One to three hours for an appliance or tap; one to two days across first and second fix

Questions about kitchen plumbing

Can I plumb in a washing machine myself?

If there is already a valved cold supply and a proper waste connection, connecting a washing machine is a simple job many people do themselves. Where new pipework, a tee into the supply or a new waste is needed, it is worth having it done properly, because a small leak behind a machine can run unseen for weeks.

Why does my kitchen sink smell?

Drain smells usually mean the trap has dried out, is the wrong type, or the waste has a build-up of grease and food. A sagging waste pipe that holds water can also cause it. Cleaning the trap often cures it; if not, we check the waste run and the connections to the appliances.

Do boiling-water taps need special plumbing?

They need a cold supply, a nearby electrical spur, space under the sink for the tank and filter, and a mains pressure within the manufacturer's range. Some need a pressure-reducing valve. Checking these before you buy avoids disappointment, and correct fitting usually matters for the manufacturer's guarantee.
