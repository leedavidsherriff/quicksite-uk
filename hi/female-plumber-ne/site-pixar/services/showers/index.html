A shower that suits your water system

Electric, mixer and pumped showers fitted, replaced and repaired.

Get my guide priceCall 07359 671340

The most common shower complaint we hear is a new shower that is disappointing on day one. That is almost always a mismatch between the shower and the water system feeding it. Before recommending anything, we look at what heats your water, how much pressure and flow reaches the bathroom, and where the pipes run. Then we fit the right type, whether that is a straightforward electric shower replacement, a thermostatic mixer off a combi boiler, or a pumped shower on a gravity system. This page explains the options so you know what you are buying and why.

Electric, mixer or power shower

An electric shower heats cold mains water itself, so it works whatever your boiler is doing and is ideal as a second shower. Performance depends on its rating, typically 8.5 to 10.5kW, and a higher rating usually needs a heavier cable back to the consumer unit. A mixer shower blends hot and cold from your existing system and gives a stronger flow on a combi or unvented cylinder. A power shower is a mixer with a built-in pump, designed for gravity systems with a cold tank in the loft. It must never be connected to mains pressure.

Why we test your pressure first

We measure the static pressure and, more usefully, the flow rate in litres per minute at the bathroom. A combi boiler producing 12 litres a minute will happily run a standard mixer, but a large rainfall head can drain that and leave the shower lukewarm. On gravity systems the height of the cold tank above the shower head decides the pressure, and many need a pump to perform well. Getting this right at the survey avoids the expensive mistake of fitting a shower your system cannot feed.

Thermostatic valves and safety

A thermostatic mixer valve holds the temperature steady when someone flushes a toilet or turns on a tap elsewhere, and cuts the flow if the cold supply fails. That matters for children and older people, because water from a cylinder can be hot enough to scald. We fit thermostatic valves as standard on new mixer installations. Concealed valves look tidy but need a service panel or accessible fixings, because cartridges eventually need replacing. Exposed bar valves are easier to maintain and cheaper to fit.

Electrical work on electric showers

Replacing an electric shower with one of the same rating on an existing, adequate circuit is usually straightforward. Fitting a higher-rated shower may mean a new, thicker cable and a new protective device at the consumer unit, and work in a bathroom is notifiable under the electrical safety rules in the Building Regulations. That part of the job has to be done or certified by a registered electrician. We check the cable size and circuit protection before quoting, and tell you clearly if an electrical upgrade is needed.

How long it takes

Half a day for a straight swap, one to two days for a new concealed shower

Questions about showers

Why is my new shower so weak?

Usually the shower does not match the water system. A mixer on a low-pressure gravity supply, or a big rainfall head on a small combi, will struggle. Scale in the head, a blocked filter in the valve, or a partly closed isolation valve can also cut flow. A flow-rate test quickly shows which one it is and what will fix it.

Can I put a power shower on a combi boiler?

No. Power showers contain a pump designed for low-pressure gravity systems, and connecting one to mains-pressure water from a combi is not allowed under the water regulations and can damage the pump. On a combi you want a thermostatic mixer shower instead, which uses the mains pressure you already have.

How long should a shower last?

A decent electric shower typically lasts around ten years, less in hard water areas unless it is descaled regularly. Thermostatic mixer valves can last much longer if the cartridge is replaced when it starts to stick. Choosing a make with spare parts readily available makes a big difference to how long you keep it.
