Render that protects the house and lifts the look

New and replacement render in traditional, silicone and monocouche systems.

Render keeps rain off masonry and can transform a tired, patchy or mismatched house into something clean and modern. It's also one of the largest jobs you can do to the outside of a home, so choosing the right system matters. We render new extensions, replace failed and cracked render, and re-render whole houses in Pontypridd, using traditional sand and cement, modern silicone thin-coat systems or through-coloured monocouche. We'll explain the maintenance, cost and look of each, and check whether planning permission or building regulations apply before any work is booked.

Sand and cement, silicone or monocouche

Traditional sand and cement render is applied in two coats and then painted with masonry paint. It's the lowest-cost option upfront but needs repainting every few years and can crack if the wall moves. Silicone thin-coat render is applied over a base coat with reinforcing mesh, comes through-coloured and is water-repellent, so rain beads off and algae struggles to grow. It rarely needs painting and self-cleans reasonably well. Monocouche is a thicker, single-coat, through-coloured render that's scraped back for a textured finish. It's cheaper than silicone but can show staining over time on exposed walls. We'll show you finished samples of each so you can compare the texture and colour in daylight.

Preparation, beads and details

Old failed render is hacked off and the wall is cleaned, with loose bricks or failed pointing repaired. Bell-cast beads are fitted just above the damp-proof course, so the render stops above ground level and doesn't bridge it, which is a common cause of rising damp complaints. Stop beads finish neatly around windows and doors, and movement beads are placed where different materials meet. Window sills may need extending so water drips clear of the new render. These details make the difference between a render that looks crisp and lasts, and one that cracks at the corners and stains below every sill.

Scaffolding, planning and timing

Most rendering needs scaffolding, which is usually a significant part of the price, so it's worth combining the job with fascias, gutters or roof repairs while it's up. Re-rendering an existing rendered house normally doesn't need planning permission, but rendering a brick house in a conservation area, or a listed building, may. If you're rendering more than a quarter of an external wall, building regulations may require the wall's insulation to be upgraded, so external wall insulation is worth considering. Rendering needs dry weather and temperatures above around five degrees, so it's normally done between spring and autumn.

How long it takes

Typically 1–2 weeks for a semi-detached house, weather permitting

Questions about external rendering

How long does render last?

A well-applied silicone or monocouche system can last for decades with little more than a wash. Sand and cement render is also durable but needs repainting every several years to keep it looking good and waterproof. The biggest factors are correct beading, a sound background and not bridging the damp-proof course.

Do I need planning permission to render my house?

Usually not if you're re-rendering or using a similar finish to other houses nearby. You may need permission in a conservation area, on a listed building or if the change is significant, such as rendering a brick front in a street of brick houses. Check with your local planning department before committing.

Can render be applied over brick?

Yes. Brick is a good background for render once it's clean and sound. Very smooth or dense bricks may need a bonding primer or a stipple coat so the render grips. Old soft bricks in solid-wall houses may be better suited to a breathable lime render, which lets moisture escape.
