Breathable lime for older homes

Lime plaster and render repairs that let old solid walls breathe and dry out.

Get my guide priceCall 07866 924600

Most houses built before about 1920 have solid walls and were built with lime mortar and lime plaster. These walls need to breathe, absorbing moisture and letting it evaporate. Cover them with gypsum plaster, cement render and plastic paints, and moisture gets trapped, leading to damp patches, crumbling plaster and mould. We repair and replace lime plaster and lime render on period cottages, chapels, terraces and farmhouses in and around Chippenham, matching the original materials so the building works as it was designed to.

Why gypsum and cement cause problems in old walls

Old solid walls have no cavity and no modern damp-proof course, so they rely on moisture evaporating from their surfaces. Gypsum plaster breaks down when damp and cement render is so dense that water gets behind it and can't escape. The result is often a damp problem that gets blamed on rising damp, when the real cause is the wrong materials. Replacing them with lime lets the wall dry out gradually. It's not a quick fix, and it works best alongside other measures like lowering outside ground levels, repairing gutters and ventilating rooms. We'll walk round the outside of the building with you too, because the cause of damp inside is often a problem outside.

Lime putty, hydraulic lime and hemp-lime

Non-hydraulic lime putty is the most breathable and flexible, and was used in most traditional interior plasterwork. It sets slowly by absorbing carbon dioxide from the air. Natural hydraulic lime, graded NHL 2, 3.5 or 5, sets partly with water and is tougher, making it suitable for external render and damp or exposed areas. Hemp-lime and insulating lime plasters add a little thermal performance while staying breathable. Lime plaster is applied in layers, often with animal hair or fibres for strength, and each coat needs to be kept damp as it cures. We choose the mix to match what's already on the wall wherever possible, so repairs behave in the same way as the original plaster.

Finishing and decorating lime

Lime plaster is finished smooth or with a slightly softer, hand-made look that suits period interiors. It needs to be decorated with breathable paints: limewash, clay paint or mineral silicate paint. Standard vinyl emulsions will seal the surface and undo much of the benefit. Lime cures more slowly than gypsum, so allow several weeks before decorating and protect it from frost and hot sun while it cures. Externally, lime render is finished with limewash, which can be tinted with natural pigments and refreshed every few years.

How long it takes

Typically 1–2 weeks for a room, with curing time between coats

Questions about lime plastering

Does my house need lime plaster?

If it was built before about 1920 with solid walls, lime is usually the right choice for repairs. Signs of trouble include blistering plaster, salts on the surface and damp patches that return after being treated. A lime specialist can check what's on the walls and whether it's causing problems.

Is lime plaster more expensive than gypsum?

Yes, usually. It's applied in more coats, takes longer to cure and needs more careful handling, so labour costs are higher. The materials themselves aren't hugely expensive. The benefit is a wall that can breathe and dry out, which often solves damp problems that gypsum repairs keep failing to fix.

Can I paint lime plaster with normal emulsion?

It's best not to. Vinyl emulsions seal the surface and stop moisture escaping, which undermines the reason for using lime. Limewash, clay paints and mineral silicate paints are all breathable and come in a wide range of colours. We can recommend suitable products for your walls.
