New lawns on properly prepared ground

Ground cleared, levelled and fed, then quality turf laid and watered in.

Get my guide priceCall 07912 690100

A new lawn is only as good as the ground underneath it. Turf rolled out over bumpy, compacted clay or builders' rubble will look fine for a fortnight and then yellow, sink in patches and fill with weeds. We clear the old grass and weeds, loosen the soil, bring in topsoil where the ground is poor, level it carefully and firm it before laying fresh turf. You get a lawn that knits in quickly, drains well and mows evenly, with clear instructions for the few weeks it needs to establish.

Preparing the ground

We start by stripping the old turf or treating weeds, then rotavate or dig the soil to loosen compaction. Stones, roots and rubble are raked out. On heavy clay or thin soil we add 50–100 mm of screened topsoil, which gives the roots something to grow into. The area is then raked level, firmed by treading or rolling to remove soft spots, raked again and given a pre-turf fertiliser. Levels are set to sit just below paths and patio edges so the mower runs cleanly over them. Poor preparation is the main reason new lawns fail.

Turf or seed

Turf gives an instant lawn you can walk on gently within a few weeks and use fully in around six to eight weeks. It is the best choice for families, dogs and anyone who wants a finished garden quickly. Seed is cheaper and offers a wider choice of grass mixes, including shade-tolerant ones, but takes months to thicken, is vulnerable to birds and heavy rain, and needs sowing in spring or early autumn. Hard-wearing turf containing ryegrass suits family gardens; finer ornamental grasses look beautiful but need much more care.

The first six weeks

Newly laid turf must not dry out. Water it thoroughly straight after laying and then every day in dry weather for the first two weeks, enough to soak through to the soil below. Lift a corner: if roots are growing into the soil, it is knitting in. Keep off it as much as possible for two to three weeks, then give the first cut with the mower set high, taking off only the tips. Turf laid in autumn or spring establishes most easily. Summer laying is fine with reliable watering; frozen or waterlogged ground is best avoided.

Drainage and problem areas

If your old lawn was always soggy, new turf will not fix that on its own. Waterlogging usually comes from compacted clay, a high water table or water running off a patio or path. Solutions range from working in sharp sand and topsoil to improve the soil, through to a French drain or a soakaway at the low point. Heavily shaded areas under trees rarely grow good grass; a shade-tolerant seed mix, bark or planting can work better. We will tell you honestly if a spot is never going to be a good lawn.

How long it takes

Typically 1–2 days for a 50 m² lawn

Questions about lawns and turf

When is the best time to lay turf?

Autumn and spring are ideal, because the soil is warm and moist and rain helps with watering. Turf can be laid all year when the ground is not frozen or waterlogged, but summer laying needs daily watering to stop it drying out and shrinking at the joins.

How soon can I walk on new turf?

Keep off it as much as possible for two to three weeks while the roots grow into the soil, apart from moving the sprinkler. After the first mow, light use is fine. Most lawns can take normal family use, including children and pets, after around six to eight weeks.

Why has my new turf gone yellow?

Yellowing in the first weeks usually means the turf has dried out, or it was left rolled up too long before laying. Water deeply and keep watering; most turf recovers. Patchy yellowing later can point to poor soil, compaction or dog urine.
