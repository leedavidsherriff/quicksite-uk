Decking built on a frame that lasts

Softwood, hardwood or composite boards on a solid, ventilated frame.

Get my guide priceCall 07912 690100

Decking is often the best way to create a level outdoor space on a sloping garden, or to step straight out from patio doors without a drop. It is quick to build and warm underfoot, but a deck is only as good as the frame beneath it. Rotten joists, springy boards and slippery surfaces nearly always come from cheap timber, joists spaced too far apart or no airflow underneath. We build frames from treated structural timber on solid footings, with the right spacing and ventilation for the boards you choose.

The frame underneath

A deck frame is built from pressure-treated structural timber, with posts and any timber in ground contact treated to UC4. Joists are usually spaced at 400 mm centres for softwood boards, and often 300–350 mm for composite, following the board manufacturer's instructions. Posts sit on concrete footings or adjustable pedestals, and the whole frame is braced so it does not rack. A weed membrane goes underneath, and we leave a gap for air to flow, because a deck that cannot breathe stays damp and rots from below. Aluminium frames are an option for composite decks where a very long life is wanted.

Softwood, hardwood or composite

Treated softwood is the most affordable and easy to work with, but it needs cleaning and oiling or staining every year or two, and it can split and splinter over time. Hardwoods such as balau are denser and longer-lasting, with a rich colour, and cost more. Composite boards, made from wood fibre and plastic, do not rot, splinter or need staining, and keep their colour well. They cost more upfront, can get warm in strong sun and need expansion gaps at ends and edges. For most people wanting low upkeep, composite is the better long-term value.

Grip and safety

Timber decks become slippery when algae builds up in shady, damp spots, which is why they need regular cleaning. Grooved softwood boards help a little; anti-slip strips or boards with grit inserts help much more on steps and walkways. Composite boards are generally more slip-resistant when kept clean. Raised decks need balustrades: a common guide is guarding once a deck is more than about 600 mm above the ground. Steps should have even risers, and handrails help on longer flights. Lighting set into steps and edges makes the deck safer after dark.

Planning permission for decking

Ground-level decking generally needs no planning permission. Decking or a raised platform more than 30 cm above the ground usually does, and there are further limits in front gardens, on listed buildings and in conservation areas. Raised decks can also overlook neighbours, which is a common cause of complaints even where permission is not needed. We keep decks under 30 cm where possible and will advise you when an application is likely, so you can check with your council before we start.

How long it takes

Typically 2–4 days for a 15–20 m² deck

Questions about decking

How long does decking last?

Treated softwood decking typically lasts ten to fifteen years with regular cleaning and oiling. Hardwood lasts twenty years or more. Good composite decking often comes with a long manufacturer's warranty and can last twenty-five years. In every case, the frame and ventilation underneath decide how long it really lasts.

Is composite decking slippery?

Most modern composite boards are more slip-resistant than timber when kept clean, because they do not grow algae as easily. Any deck in a shady, damp spot can become slippery if leaves and dirt build up, so an occasional wash with a soft brush helps.

Do I need planning permission for a deck?

Usually not if it is at ground level. Decking or a raised platform more than 30 cm above the ground generally needs planning permission, and front gardens, listed buildings and conservation areas have extra rules. We will tell you if we think you need to apply.
