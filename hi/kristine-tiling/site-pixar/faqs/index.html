Questions

Ask me anything.

The things people ask most, plus what the words on your quote actually mean.

Do you give free quotes?

Yes. We visit, measure, check the walls and floors and send a written quote with preparation, tiling and materials itemised. Small jobs can often be quoted from photos.

Do you supply the tiles?

Most customers choose their own tiles. We'll advise on quantities and suitability, and supply adhesive, grout, trims and boards to suit the tile.

How do I pay?

For larger jobs we may ask for a deposit for materials, with the balance on completion. Small jobs are paid on completion. You'll get an itemised invoice.

Are you insured?

Any tiler working in your home should carry public liability insurance. Ask us for our insurance details and we'll send them before we start.

Can you work with my plumber and electrician?

Yes. Tiling usually goes after first-fix plumbing and electrics, and before fitting sanitaryware and fronts. We'll agree a sequence with your other trades.

How much mess is there?

Removing old tiles is dusty and noisy. We sheet floors, seal doorways, use dust extraction when cutting, and clean up at the end of each day.

How much extra tile should I order?

Usually ten to fifteen per cent for straight layouts, and more for herringbone or diagonal. Keep a few spares from the same batch for future repairs.

Is underfloor heating worth it with tiles?

Tiles conduct heat well, so it works efficiently. Electric mats suit single rooms. The final connection must be made by a qualified electrician.

Jargon buster

Words on your quote.

Tanking
Waterproofing walls and floors with a membrane before tiling, essential in showers and wet rooms.
Setting out
Planning where full tiles and cuts will fall before fixing any tiles.
Lippage
When one tile edge sits higher than the next, most visible with large tiles.
Backer board
A cement-based or waterproof board used instead of plasterboard for tiling in wet areas.
S1 adhesive
A flexible, deformable tile adhesive used for porcelain, large tiles and floors that move slightly.
Back-buttering
Spreading adhesive on the back of a tile as well as the wall or floor to get full coverage.
Decoupling membrane
A layer between floor and tiles that absorbs movement and helps prevent cracking.
Epoxy grout
A resin-based grout that is harder wearing and more stain-resistant than cement grout.
Movement joint
A flexible joint in tiling that lets the floor or wall expand without cracking tiles.
Third offset
Laying rectangular tiles so each row overlaps by a third, reducing lippage with bowed tiles.
