Big tiles, fewer joints, a flawless finish

Large porcelain tiles and slabs laid flat and flush on floors and walls.

Get my guide priceCall 07925 753336

Large-format porcelain, from 60 × 120 cm tiles up to full-height slabs, gives floors and walls a clean, almost seamless look with very few grout lines. It's popular in open-plan kitchens, bathrooms and living spaces, and can run seamlessly from inside to outside with matching outdoor tiles. But big tiles are unforgiving: any unevenness in the floor or wall shows as lippage, where one tile edge sits higher than the next. We fit large-format tiles for homeowners in Wigan with the preparation, tools and handling equipment they need.

Why flatness matters so much

The bigger the tile, the flatter the surface needs to be. A dip that a small tile would follow will leave a large tile bridging across it, creating hollow areas and lippage at the joints. Floors often need self-levelling compound and walls may need re-skimming or boarding before large tiles go on. Large porcelain tiles are also slightly bowed from manufacture, which is why many manufacturers recommend a third-offset rather than half-offset brick pattern. We use levelling clip systems to hold edges flush while the adhesive sets, which gives a flat, even surface. On floors, we check flatness with a long straightedge and mark any high or low spots for grinding or levelling.

Adhesive, coverage and handling

Large tiles need close to full adhesive coverage, with no voids underneath that could lead to cracking. We use a deformable S1 or S2 adhesive, combed with a large notched trowel and back-buttered onto each tile. Tiles are lifted with suction cups and, for slabs, a handling frame, to avoid snapping. Cuts for sockets, drains and pipes are made with specialist cutters and wet saws, and a single broken slab can be expensive, so we plan cuts carefully. Large wall tiles and slabs are heavy, so the wall must be able to carry the weight. Joints are kept narrow, often around two to three millimetres, which suits the clean look but needs accurate tiles and a careful setting out.

Where large-format works best

Large-format floors look impressive in open-plan kitchen diners, where fewer grout lines make the space feel larger. In bathrooms, large wall tiles mean fewer joints to clean and a sleek, hotel-style finish. Slabs can wrap a shower, run behind a bath or create a feature wall with continuous veining. In very small rooms or with lots of doorways and cut-outs, big tiles can mean more waste and awkward cuts, so a mid-size tile may be the better choice. We'll give an honest view on whether large-format suits your room. Matching outdoor tiles, usually 20 mm thick, can continue the same line through bifold or sliding doors onto a patio.

Questions about large-format tiles

What is lippage?

Lippage is when the edge of one tile sits higher than the one next to it. It's more noticeable with large tiles, and in raking light from windows. It's usually caused by uneven floors or bowed tiles. A flat base, a third-offset pattern and a levelling clip system all help to keep lippage to a minimum.

Are large tiles more expensive to fit?

Usually, yes, per square metre. They need a flatter base, more adhesive, more careful handling and often two people. But there are fewer joints to grout, and the finished look is very clean. For open-plan spaces, many people feel the extra fitting cost is worth it.

Can large-format tiles go on walls?

Yes, as long as the wall is flat and strong enough. A 60 × 120 cm porcelain tile plus adhesive can be heavy, which rules out some plaster walls. Cement backer boards or suitable plasterboard are often used, and we check weight limits and fixings before tiling.
