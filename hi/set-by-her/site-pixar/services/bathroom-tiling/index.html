Bathroom tiling that looks sharp and keeps water out

The tiles are what everyone sees in a bathroom, but the work that matters most is underneath them: a flat, strong background, waterproofing where water will hit, and joints that are sealed properly. We tile full bathrooms, feature walls, bath surrounds and floors for homeowners in Warrington, working with ceramic, porcelain, natural stone and handmade tiles. We can work alongside your plumber and electrician or as part of a full refit, and you'll get a quote that shows the preparation, the tiling and any trims or extras separately.

Setting out: where the cuts land

Good tiling starts before a single tile goes on. We measure each wall and plan where the full tiles and cuts will fall, so you don't end up with a thin sliver of tile at the ceiling, in a corner or around the window. Lines are centred on the most visible wall, the bath or the basin, and a level datum is marked rather than trusting the bath or floor to be level, which they rarely are. Patterns such as brick bond, stacked, vertical or herringbone are laid out on paper first, and we'll show you where the cuts will fall before we fix anything. Around the window, reveals are tiled so the cuts are even on both sides.

Backgrounds, weight limits and waterproofing

Walls must be flat, sound and strong enough for the tiles. Gypsum plaster can generally carry tile and adhesive up to about 20 kg per square metre, and unskimmed plasterboard about 32 kg, which rules out some heavy natural stone and large porcelain on plaster. In showers, cement-based backer board or waterproof boards are better than plasterboard. Any area that gets direct spray is tanked with a liquid membrane or waterproof sheet system, with tape at corners and collars around pipes. Tiles and grout are not waterproof on their own, so skipping this step is the main reason tiled showers leak into ceilings below.

Adhesive, grout, trims and silicone

Porcelain is dense and needs a flexible cement-based adhesive, often labelled S1, and larger tiles need full-bed coverage with no hollow spots. Levelling clips help keep large tiles flush. Grout colour makes a big difference to the final look, from a matching tone that blends in to a contrast that shows off the pattern. Epoxy grout is harder wearing and stain-resistant, which suits shower floors. External corners are finished with metal or PVC trims, or mitred edges on thicker porcelain. Internal corners and the bath or tray joint are sealed with sanitary silicone, never grout, because these joints move.

Questions about bathroom tiling

How much does it cost to tile a bathroom?

For an average family bathroom, labour for tiling the walls and floor usually falls around the full-bathroom price shown here, plus the cost of the tiles, adhesive and grout. The final figure depends on tile size, pattern, how many cuts there are, and whether walls need boarding or tanking first. We'll give a written breakdown after a visit.

Can you tile over existing tiles?

Sometimes, if the old tiles are firmly bonded, flat and the wall can take the extra weight. It saves time, but it thickens the wall, can cause problems around fittings and hides any issues underneath. In showers and wet areas we recommend removing old tiles so the background can be checked and properly waterproofed.

Do I need to buy the tiles?

Usually, yes. Most customers choose their own tiles from a showroom or supplier. We'll work out the quantity with allowance for cuts and breakages, typically around ten to fifteen per cent extra, and more for herringbone or diagonal layouts. We can supply adhesive, grout, trims and boards so they suit the tile.
