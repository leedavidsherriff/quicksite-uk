Not every tired bathroom needs to be ripped out. Often the tiles are fine, but the grout is stained or crumbling, the silicone is black with mould, or a couple of tiles have cracked. We regrout, reseal and repair tiling for homeowners and landlords in Warrington, making bathrooms and kitchens look fresh for a fraction of the cost of new tiles. It's also important maintenance: failing grout and silicone let water behind the tiles, which is how small problems become leaks through the ceiling. We'll be honest if the tiling has gone too far and needs replacing.

Regrouting: what's involved

Old grout is raked out to a consistent depth with a grout rake or oscillating tool, taking care not to chip the tile edges. The joints are vacuumed and cleaned, then filled with fresh grout, which is pressed in firmly and tooled flush. We can match your existing colour or change it, which can give tiling a completely new look. For showers and high-wear areas, an epoxy or high-performance grout resists staining and mould much better than standard cement grout. Regrouting takes longer than people expect, but it's far quicker and cheaper than re-tiling.

Replacing silicone and loose or cracked tiles

Mouldy or peeling silicone is cut out completely, with every trace removed, then the joint is cleaned and treated with a mould remover and allowed to dry. New sanitary silicone with fungicide is applied and tooled smooth. Baths should be filled with water before sealing, so the joint doesn't split when the bath flexes under weight. Cracked or loose tiles are carefully cut out without damaging neighbours, the adhesive behind is cleaned off and a replacement is fitted. Finding a matching tile is often the hardest part, so if you have spares in the loft, keep them. If the bath is loose or drops when you step in, it's secured with extra support before resealing, or the new silicone will split again.

When repair isn't enough

If tiles sound hollow across large areas, if water is coming through ceilings below, or if the wall behind feels soft when pressed, the problem is behind the tiles and regrouting won't fix it. In showers without tanking, water may have soaked into plasterboard. In those cases we'll explain what we've found and give you a quote for stripping out and re-tiling properly. For landlords, a regrout and reseal between tenancies is a cheap way to keep bathrooms looking good and catch problems early. We'll photograph anything we find so you can see exactly what's going on.

How long it takes

Typically half a day for a reseal; 1–2 days for a full bathroom regrout

Questions about regrouting and tile repairs

Can you regrout over old grout?

Not properly. New grout needs depth to bond, so the old grout must be raked out to at least a few millimetres. Spreading fresh grout over the top will crack and flake off quickly. Grout pens and paints can temporarily improve the colour, but they don't fix crumbling or porous grout.

How long does silicone last in a shower?

Good-quality sanitary silicone can last several years, but in a busy shower it may discolour or lose adhesion sooner. Regular cleaning, good ventilation and wiping down after showering help it last. If it's peeling, black inside or letting water through, it should be removed and replaced.

What if I don't have a matching tile?

We can sometimes find a match from suppliers or salvage, especially for common tiles. If not, one option is to take a tile from a less visible spot, such as behind a bath panel, and replace that with a close match. Another is to create a deliberate contrast feature.
