Splashbacks that finish the kitchen off

Metro, zellige, patterned and large-format splashbacks fitted around sockets and worktops.

Get my guide priceCall 07552 897039

A tiled splashback protects the wall behind the hob and sink, but it's also one of the quickest ways to change the look of a kitchen. We tile splashbacks for new kitchens and for kitchens that just need a refresh, in Warrington, using everything from classic white metro tiles to handmade zellige, patterned encaustic-style tiles and large porcelain slabs. It's usually a one or two-day job and, with good planning around sockets, windows and wall units, the result looks built-in rather than added on. We'll help with tile quantities and layout before you order.

Planning the layout around the kitchen

Splashbacks are full of interruptions: sockets, switches, the cooker hood, window reveals and the gap between worktop and wall units. We plan the layout so full tiles sit on the most visible lines, typically centred on the hob or the sink, and cuts fall at the ends and under wall units. The first row sits on a level batten rather than directly on the worktop, which is rarely level, and a silicone joint is left at the bottom. Socket and switch plates are loosened by a competent person so the tiles tuck neatly underneath and the fronts sit flush. Behind the sink, any gaps around pipes and taps are sealed before tiling so splashes can't get into the wall.

Tile styles and what they involve

Metro and brick-shaped tiles are timeless and forgiving, and can be laid brick bond, stacked or vertically. Herringbone metro needs more cutting and time. Handmade zellige and glazed tiles have beautiful variation, but their uneven edges and thicknesses need a wider joint and a skilled hand to look right. Mosaics on sheets are quick to fit on flat areas but fiddly around sockets. Large-format porcelain gives a near-seamless finish but needs a very flat wall. Behind a gas hob, check the manufacturer's clearances; heat-resistant tiles and grout suit the area around the cooker.

Grout, sealing and keeping it clean

Kitchen splashbacks get grease and food splashes, so grout choice matters. A mid-tone or darker grout hides staining better than bright white, and an epoxy or stain-resistant grout is worth considering behind the hob. Handmade and natural stone tiles may need sealing before and after grouting to stop the grout staining the tile face. The joint where the splashback meets the worktop is finished with a flexible sanitary silicone, which allows for small movements and is easy to replace if it discolours. We'll leave cleaning advice specific to your tile and grout. If you ever need to replace a single tile, a neat grout line and spare tiles from the same batch make it a quick repair.

How long it takes

Typically 1–2 days, including grouting

Questions about kitchen splashbacks

How high should a kitchen splashback be?

A common choice is to tile from the worktop up to the underside of the wall units, which is usually about 45 to 60 cm. Behind the hob, many people tile up to the cooker hood. Where there are no wall units, tiling to a full tile height or to the ceiling gives a clean, modern look.

Can you tile a splashback before the worktop is fitted?

It's best to tile after the worktops are in, so the tiles sit neatly on a batten just above the worktop and the joint can be sealed with silicone. If worktops are being replaced later, the bottom row can be left off and fitted afterwards, but that adds another visit.

Are handmade tiles harder to fit?

They take more time and care. Handmade and zellige tiles vary in size, thickness and edge shape, so they're laid with wider joints and more attention to keep the face even. The variation is part of their appeal, but the price per square metre is higher than for regular machine-made tiles.
