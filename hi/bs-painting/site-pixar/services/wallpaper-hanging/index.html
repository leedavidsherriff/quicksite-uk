Wallpaper hung straight, matched and seamless

Stripping, lining and hanging, from a single feature wall to a whole room.

Get my guide priceCall 07585 774145

Wallpaper is back in a big way, but it is far less forgiving than paint. Walls that look fine in emulsion show every bump under a flat paper, and a pattern that drifts a few millimetres by the corner is obvious forever. We strip old paper, repair and line walls where they need it, and hang everything from plain lining and paintable textures to bold patterns, murals and grasscloth, for homes in Pencoed. Tell us what you've chosen and we'll check the roll quantity, the pattern repeat and the adhesive it needs before you order.

Stripping and preparing the walls

Old paper is soaked or steamed off, taking care not to tear the plaster face beneath, which is common on older walls and plasterboard that was never sealed before papering. Any damage is filled and sanded. Bare plaster is sized or primed so the new paper slides into position and can be removed cleanly in future. On uneven or patched walls we recommend a lining paper, hung horizontally, which gives a smooth, even surface and helps the finish paper dry without shrinking at the seams. It adds a day but makes a visible difference, particularly with plain or metallic papers.

Paste-the-wall, paste-the-paper and specialist papers

Most modern non-woven wallpapers are paste-the-wall, which is quicker and less likely to stretch. Traditional papers need pasting and a soaking time, and getting that time consistent is important, or drops expand differently and the seams open. Vinyls in kitchens and bathrooms need a heavy-duty adhesive. Grasscloth, silk and hand-printed papers are hung with extra care because seams are visible by nature and the face marks easily. Murals are laid out from a centre line so the key part of the image sits where you want it. We read the manufacturer's instructions for every paper; they do differ. Where a paper needs lining first, we let the lining dry fully overnight before the finish paper goes on.

Pattern repeats and how much to order

A large pattern repeat can waste a surprising amount of paper, so roll counts are worked out from the actual wall heights and the repeat, not a generic chart. Always buy rolls from the same batch number, because shades vary slightly between print runs, and keep one spare for repairs. We plan where the first drop goes so the pattern is centred on the chimney breast or feature wall and the unavoidable mismatch falls in the least visible corner, usually behind the door. Around sockets, switches and radiators, covers are loosened so the paper tucks neatly behind. If the room has a sloping ceiling or a stairwell, tell us early, as those areas need extra paper and more time to hang cleanly.

How long it takes

Typically 1 day for a feature wall, 2–3 days to strip, line and paper a bedroom

Questions about wallpaper hanging

Do I need lining paper?

If your walls are smooth, sound and freshly painted, often not. If they are patched, uneven, have been stripped several times or you've chosen a plain, dark or metallic paper, lining makes a real difference. It evens out the surface and helps the finish paper dry evenly so the seams stay tight. We'll look at the walls and advise honestly.

How many rolls of wallpaper do I need?

It depends on the wall heights, the width of the room and the pattern repeat. A large drop repeat can add a roll or two. Send us the paper details or a link and we'll work out the quantity from your measurements, then add one spare roll from the same batch so any future repair matches.

Can you wallpaper over existing paper?

We don't recommend it. The moisture in fresh paste can loosen the old paper beneath, causing bubbles and lifting seams, and old vinyl surfaces don't take adhesive well. Stripping back to a sound, sized surface is the only way to guarantee a lasting finish, and it makes the next change much easier.
