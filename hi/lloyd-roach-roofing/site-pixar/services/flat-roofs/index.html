Flat roofs that stay dry

EPDM, GRP or torch-on felt on a new deck with proper falls and insulation.

Get my guide priceCall 07811 339098

Flat roofs on garages, extensions and dormers have a poor reputation because so many were built with a single layer of cheap felt and no real fall. Modern systems are far better. EPDM rubber, GRP fibreglass and high-performance torch-on felt all give a genuinely long-lasting, watertight roof when they are laid on a sound deck with enough slope for water to run off. We replace the whole build, not just the top layer, so you are not left with rotten boards or ponding water under a shiny new surface.

EPDM, GRP or felt — which suits you

EPDM is a single sheet of synthetic rubber, usually with no joints on a domestic roof. It copes well with temperature changes, is quick to lay and typically lasts twenty to thirty years. GRP is fibreglass matting set in resin, giving a hard, seamless surface you can walk on occasionally and that forms neat details around rooflights; it lasts twenty-five years or more but must be laid in dry weather. Modern torch-on felt is laid in two or three layers of polyester-reinforced membrane with a mineral cap sheet, and lasts around fifteen to twenty-five years. It is the most affordable and is easy to repair in patches.

The deck and the falls

Under the waterproofing, the boards need to be sound and dry. We replace old chipboard with 18 mm OSB3 or exterior plywood, and check the joists for rot. Flat roofs are never truly flat: they should be designed with a fall of about 1 in 40 so that, allowing for settling and building tolerances, the finished roof still sheds water at 1 in 80 or better. Firring strips, tapered timbers fixed over the joists, create this fall. Standing puddles, known as ponding, speed up wear on any membrane and are a sign the falls were not built in.

Warm roofs, insulation and the rules

Replacing more than a quarter of a flat roof is controlled building work, and usually means the roof must be insulated to current standards where practical. The best method for most homes is a warm roof, where rigid insulation boards go on top of the deck with a vapour control layer beneath and the waterproofing on top. This keeps the timber warm and dry and avoids condensation in the ceiling void. It raises the roof height slightly, so we check upstands, door thresholds and flashings. Ask us how your roof will be signed off.

Edges, gutters and details

Most flat roof leaks start at the edges, not the middle. We fit proper drip trims at the gutter edge, raised trims on the other sides to stop water spilling off, and upstands at least 150 mm up any adjoining wall, covered with lead or a cover flashing chased into the brickwork. Rooflights and vents are sealed with manufacturer-approved details. We finish by checking the gutter and downpipe can handle the water now being shed, because a new roof pouring into a blocked or undersized gutter simply moves the leak elsewhere.

How long it takes

Typically 1–2 days for a single garage roof; 2–4 days for an extension

Questions about flat roofs

How long does a flat roof last?

A modern EPDM or GRP flat roof typically lasts twenty to thirty years, and good three-layer torch-on felt fifteen to twenty-five years. Old single-layer felt often lasted only 10. The biggest factors are the quality of the deck, the fall, the edge details and whether water is left ponding on the surface.

Can you lay a new flat roof over the old one?

Occasionally, on a sound roof, an overlay is possible. In most cases we recommend stripping it off, because trapped moisture and rotten boards under the old covering will ruin a new one. Stripping also lets us fix the fall and add insulation properly.

Why is water pooling on my flat roof?

Ponding usually means the roof has too little fall, the deck has sagged between joists, or an outlet is blocked. Small puddles that dry within a couple of days are common, but larger standing water shortens the life of any membrane. Replacing the deck with firring strips solves it.
