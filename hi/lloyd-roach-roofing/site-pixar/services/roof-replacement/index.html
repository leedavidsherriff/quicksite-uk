Every roof reaches a point where repairs stop making sense. Slates slip faster than they can be refixed, felt crumbles when you touch it in the loft, battens are soft and rotten, or the ridge is loose along its whole length. A re-roof strips the covering back to the rafters and rebuilds it with a breathable membrane, new treated battens and either your original slates, where they are sound, or new ones. It is a big job and a big cost, so we show you the evidence that it is needed and explain every choice before you commit.

Choosing slate or tile

Concrete interlocking tiles are the most affordable covering, quick to lay and last around forty to sixty years. Natural Spanish slate is the most common slate in the UK; good grades last sixty to a hundred years and suit most older houses. UK-quarried natural slate is the premium choice, extremely durable at a hundred years or more, and is often expected on older properties and in conservation areas. Clay plain tiles weather beautifully and last a long time, but need more tiles per square metre and a steeper pitch. Swapping slate for heavier concrete tiles can overload old roof timbers, so we check the structure before recommending a change of covering.

What a modern re-roof includes

Once the old covering is stripped, we check the rafters and replace or strengthen any rotten or cracked timbers. A breathable underlay goes on next, which lets moisture escape from the loft and reduces condensation compared with old bitumen felt. New treated battens are fixed at the right gauge for the chosen slate or tile. Under the current British Standard, BS 5534, ridges, hips and verges must be mechanically fixed, not just bedded on mortar, and tiles need clips and nails appropriate to your area's wind exposure. We finish with new lead flashings where needed, and eaves protection trays so the membrane does not rot at the gutter line.

Building regulations, bats and planning

Replacing more than a quarter of a roof's covering is controlled building work. It must either be signed off by building control or self-certified by a roofer registered with a competent person scheme, and it can trigger a requirement to improve the roof's insulation where practical. Ask us for our registration details and how your job will be signed off. Bats are protected by law, and roof work can disturb roosts; if there is any sign of bats, a survey is needed before starting. In conservation areas or on listed buildings, changing the roof material usually needs consent.

How long it takes

Typically 1–2 weeks for a semi-detached house, weather permitting

Questions about roof replacement

How do I know if I need a new roof?

Signs include slates slipping across several areas, crumbling or torn felt visible in the loft, daylight through the roof, soft or sagging battens, and a ridge that is loose along its length. A roof over sixty years old with original iron nails is often close to the end of its life. We will show you photos of what we find.

Can you reuse my old slates?

Often, yes. Good-quality natural slate can outlive two or three sets of nails. We strip carefully, sort the slates and reuse the sound ones, topping up with matching reclaimed or new slate. Concrete tiles are also sometimes reusable, but clay and cheap slate may be too brittle to go back on.

Do I need building regulations approval for a new roof?

Replacing more than 25% of the roof covering is controlled building work. It must be approved by building control or self-certified by a roofer in a competent person scheme, and it may require extra insulation. Ask any roofer how they will sign your job off.
