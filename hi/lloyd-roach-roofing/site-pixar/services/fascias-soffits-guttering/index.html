Fascias, soffits and gutters, fitted the right way

Full roofline replacement in uPVC, with rotten timber removed and gutters that cope.

Get my guide priceCall 07811 339098

The fascia boards along the edge of your roof carry the gutters and hold the bottom row of tiles in place, and the soffits underneath close off the eaves. When old timber rots, gutters sag, leak and pour water down the walls, and birds and wasps move into the gaps. Replacing the whole roofline in maintenance-free uPVC ends the painting cycle and is a good moment to sort ventilation and gutter sizing too. We remove the old boards rather than hiding them, so you are not sealing rot behind a plastic cover.

Full replacement or cap-over

Cap-over, sometimes called cladding, means fixing thin uPVC boards over the existing timber. It is cheaper and quicker, and acceptable where the timber is genuinely sound. The problem is that many cap-over jobs go over rotten fascias, which keep decaying out of sight until gutters sag and the boards fail. Full replacement removes the old timber, checks the rafter ends behind it, and fixes thick 16–18 mm uPVC fascias directly to the rafters. We will look behind your existing boards and tell you honestly which approach your roof needs.

Ventilation and eaves protection

Many loft condensation problems start at the eaves. Soffits with built-in vents, or over-fascia vents, allow air to flow into the loft and out at the ridge, which keeps timbers dry. Where insulation is pushed right into the eaves, rafter trays keep an air gap open. The bottom of old roofing felt often rots where it hangs into the gutter, leaving the ends of the rafters wet; an eaves protection tray under the first row of tiles fixes that permanently. These are small parts but they make a real difference to how long the roof lasts.

Gutters that cope with modern rain

Standard 112 mm half-round gutters suit most homes, but larger roofs, steep pitches or areas with heavy downpours benefit from deep-flow or high-capacity profiles. Ogee and square profiles are a matter of looks. Gutters need a slight fall towards the outlet, joints that are clipped and sealed properly, and enough downpipes for the length of run. Cast iron gutters are often expected on listed buildings and in conservation areas; cast-iron-effect uPVC or aluminium are lower-maintenance alternatives elsewhere. We also check that downpipes discharge into a working drain or soakaway.

Scaffold, colours and timing

Roofline work is normally done from scaffold or a tower, which is safer and gives a better result than working off ladders. If you are planning roof repairs, windows or rendering, doing the fascias at the same time shares the scaffold cost. White is still the most popular colour, but anthracite grey, black and wood-effect finishes are widely available to match modern windows. Most houses take one to three days, and the work is done from outside, so you do not need to be at home the whole time.

Questions about fascias, soffits and guttering

How long do uPVC fascias last?

Good-quality uPVC fascias and soffits typically last twenty to thirty years or more without painting, just an occasional wash. Their life depends on proper fixing to sound rafters. Thin cap-over boards fixed over rotten timber can fail far sooner, because the timber behind them keeps decaying.

Why do my gutters overflow?

The usual reasons are blockages from moss and leaves, gutters with no fall or a sag, too few downpipes, or gutters too small for the roof. A clean and realignment fixes most problems. If they overflow even when clear, a deep-flow profile or an extra downpipe is the answer.

Do I need scaffolding for fascias?

For most two-storey houses, yes, or at least a tower. Replacing fascias involves removing the bottom row of tiles, handling long boards and working along the whole eaves, which is not safe from a ladder. The scaffold cost will be listed separately on your quote.
