Roof repairs that find the real leak

Slipped slates, broken tiles, leaks and storm damage, found and fixed properly.

Get my guide priceCall 07811 339098

Most roofs do not need replacing; they need a good roofer to find the actual problem. A damp patch on a bedroom ceiling is rarely directly below the hole, because water runs along felt, battens and rafters before it drips. We inspect the roof and, where we can, the loft, trace the leak back to its source and fix that, rather than slapping sealant over anything that looks suspicious. You get photos of the problem and the repair, and an honest view on whether the roof has plenty of life left or is starting to fail more widely.

The usual causes of a leaking roof

On older slate roofs the most common problem is nail sickness: the original iron nails rust through and slates start slipping one by one. On tiled roofs it is often cracked or displaced tiles after high winds, or ridge tiles whose mortar has crumbled. Other regular culprits are lead flashings around chimneys and walls that have split or lifted, blocked valley gutters where two slopes meet, and gutters overflowing back under the eaves. Condensation in a poorly ventilated loft can also look like a leak, so we check for that before assuming the covering has failed.

How slipped slates and tiles are fixed

A single slipped slate is usually refixed with a tingle, a small strip of copper or lead nailed between the slates below and folded up to hold the slipped one in place. It is a standard, long-lasting repair. Broken concrete or clay tiles are lifted out and replaced with matching ones, and neighbouring tiles are checked for loose nibs or clips. Where many slates are slipping across a slope, that points to widespread nail sickness, and piecemeal repairs become a false economy. In that case we will tell you, and quote for stripping and relaying that slope on new battens and membrane.

Safe access: ladders, towers and scaffold

Working at height regulations mean ladders are only suitable for short, low-risk jobs such as refixing a few slates on a single-storey or easy two-storey roof. For anything longer, higher or on a steep pitch, we use a tower or scaffold, and that cost will be shown separately on your quote so you know what you are paying for. Scaffold on the pavement usually needs a council licence, which the scaffold firm arranges. If you already have scaffold up for other work, such as rendering or windows, it is the ideal time to get the roof checked.

Emergency and storm repairs

After a storm, the priority is to stop water getting in. Where we cannot make a permanent repair straight away, we can fit a temporary cover or weatherproof the area to protect ceilings and wiring, then return to complete the job properly. Take photos of any damage for your insurer before anything is moved, and keep receipts. Many home insurance policies cover sudden storm damage but not wear and tear, so our written report describing what we found can help with your claim.

Questions about roof repairs

Why is my roof leaking when it isn't raining?

Water appearing on dry days is often condensation, from warm damp air rising into a cold, poorly ventilated loft. It can also be a slow leak from a water tank or pipe. We check ventilation, insulation and plumbing in the loft before assuming the roof covering has failed.

Is a repair or a new roof better value?

If the problem is local, such as a few slipped slates, a cracked tile or a failed flashing, a repair is almost always the better value. If slates are slipping across whole slopes, the battens are rotten or the felt has perished, repeated repairs cost more over a few years than relaying the roof.

Will my insurance cover a roof repair?

Most policies cover sudden damage from storms or falling trees, but not gradual wear and tear such as nail sickness or perished felt. Take photos before any work, keep our written report and check your policy wording. Your insurer may want to see the report before approving a claim.
