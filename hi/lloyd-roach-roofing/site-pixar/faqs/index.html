Questions

Ask me anything.

The things people ask most, plus what the words on your quote actually mean.

Do you give free roof inspections?

We give free quotes and look at the roof from the ground, the loft where possible, and from a ladder where it is safe. For a detailed report on a hard-to-reach roof, access may be needed, and we will tell you the cost beforehand.

Do you take a deposit?

Small repairs are paid on completion. For larger jobs such as re-roofs we may ask for a modest deposit to order materials and book scaffold, with the balance due when the job is finished. It is all set out in the written quote.

Are you insured?

Yes, we carry public liability insurance and can show you the certificate before work starts. Scaffold firms carry their own insurance too. Always ask any roofer for proof of cover before letting them on your roof.

Will you show me what's wrong?

Yes. We take photos of the problem before we start and of the finished repair, so you can see exactly what was done on a part of your house you cannot easily see yourself.

What if it rains during the job?

On re-roofs we only strip what can be made watertight the same day, and we cover open areas overnight. Flat roof membranes and resins need dry conditions, so we may move a start date to protect the work.

Do I need building regulations for roof work?

Replacing more than 25% of a roof covering is controlled work, signed off by building control or a competent person scheme, and may require extra insulation. Small repairs do not need approval. Ask us for our registration details.

Do you guarantee your work?

Yes. Every job comes with a written workmanship guarantee, with the length set out in your quote, and materials carry the manufacturer's own warranty. Small repairs on an old roof are guaranteed for the repair itself, not the rest of the roof.

Will there be much mess?

Roofing creates debris, so we use skips or chutes, protect paths and plants below, and clear gutters and the ground at the end of each day. We also run a magnet sweep for nails on drives and lawns when we finish.

Jargon buster

Words on your quote.

Nail sickness
When the iron nails holding slates rust through, causing slates to slip.
Tingle
A small copper or lead strip used to hold a refixed slate in place.
Flashing
Lead or similar sheet that seals the joint between the roof and a wall or chimney.
Flaunching
The sloped mortar around chimney pots that sheds rainwater.
Ridge
The row of tiles along the top where two roof slopes meet.
Verge
The edge of the roof at a gable end.
Fascia
The board along the bottom edge of the roof that carries the gutter.
Soffit
The board underneath the fascia that closes off the eaves.
Breathable membrane
The modern underlay beneath tiles that keeps rain out but lets moisture escape from the loft.
Battens
Treated timber strips fixed across the rafters that slates and tiles hang from.
BS 5534
The British Standard for slating and tiling, including how tiles must be fixed against wind.
Warm roof
A flat roof with insulation laid on top of the deck, under the waterproofing.
