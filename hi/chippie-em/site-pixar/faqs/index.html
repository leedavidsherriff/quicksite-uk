Questions

Ask me anything.

The things people ask most, plus what the words on your quote actually mean.

Do you give free quotes?

Yes. We visit, measure and talk through what you want, then send a written quote. For fitted furniture you'll also get a drawing before anything is made.

Do you supply materials?

Yes, we can supply everything, from doors and flooring to MDF, timber and hardware. If you've chosen your own doors or flooring, we'll fit those instead.

How do I pay?

For fitted furniture and larger jobs we usually ask for a deposit before making, with the balance on completion. Small jobs are paid when finished.

Are you insured?

Any carpenter working in your home should carry public liability insurance. Ask us for our insurance details and we'll send them before we start.

How much mess is there?

Most fitted furniture is built in a workshop, so on-site mess is limited. When we cut on site we use dust extraction, protect floors and tidy up daily.

Can you work with other trades?

Yes. We often work alongside electricians, plasterers and decorators. We'll agree a sequence so sockets, lighting and painting happen at the right time.

Do you paint the finished work?

We can supply units primed or fully painted. Many customers have their decorator finish the room at the same time, so the woodwork and walls match.

How long does fitted furniture take?

Typically two to four weeks from sign-off to fitting, depending on the design and finish, with one to three days on site to install.

Jargon buster

Words on your quote.

Scribing
Cutting a panel or moulding to the exact shape of an uneven wall, floor or ceiling.
Carcass
The main box of a cupboard or wardrobe, before doors and drawers are fitted.
MDF
Medium-density fibreboard, a smooth, stable board used for painted furniture.
Second fix
Carpentry done after plastering, like skirting, architrave and doors.
Architrave
The moulding around a door or window frame that hides the joint with the wall.
Newel post
The large post at the top, bottom or turn of a staircase that supports the handrail.
Balustrade
The handrail, spindles and posts that form the safety barrier on stairs and landings.
Engineered wood
Flooring with a real hardwood top layer bonded to a stable core.
Expansion gap
A gap left around the edge of a wood floor so it can move without buckling.
Intumescent seal
A strip in a fire door or frame that swells in heat to seal the gap.
Mitre
A joint cut at an angle, usually 45 degrees, to form a neat corner.
