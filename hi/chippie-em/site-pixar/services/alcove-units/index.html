Alcoves turned into storage that looks original

Made-to-measure cupboards and shelves either side of the chimney breast.

Chimney-breast alcoves are some of the most useful but most wasted spaces in British homes. Built-in alcove units, with cupboards below and shelves above, can hide toys, games consoles, paperwork and cables, while giving a place for books, plants and ornaments. We design and build alcove units and shelving for living rooms, dining rooms and bedrooms across Pontypridd, in period and modern styles. Because every alcove is a slightly different size, and rarely square, each unit is made to measure and scribed to fit, so it looks like it's always been there.

Cupboards, shelves and media units

The most popular design is a low cupboard, usually around 80 to 90 cm high, with a solid top, and open shelves above. The cupboards can hold anything from games to paperwork and can be fitted with adjustable shelves, drawers or ventilated doors for AV equipment. Shelves are typically spaced 30 to 35 cm apart, which suits most books and ornaments. For a TV wall, we can plan cable routes, sockets and space for a soundbar, with the TV either on the chimney breast or built into the unit. Recessed LED lighting can highlight shelves in the evening. If there's a radiator or an awkward socket in the alcove, we'll design around it rather than leave a gap.

Floating shelves and shelving strength

If you prefer a lighter look, floating shelves give a clean, modern line with no visible brackets. They're typically made from solid timber or a hollow box section fitted over a steel or timber bearer fixed deep into the wall. How much weight they'll take depends on the wall: solid brick and block walls can carry heavy books, while plasterboard walls need fixings into studs or timber noggins. Thick shelves of 40 to 50 mm look substantial and resist sagging. For long shelves, a timber batten along the back wall adds support.

Styles to suit the room

In Victorian and Edwardian houses, shaker or panelled doors with a moulded top and a traditional cornice suit the period. In modern homes, flat slab doors, push-to-open catches and floating shelves give a minimal look. Fluted or reeded doors are a popular modern-classic option, and cane-panel doors bring texture. Units are usually painted to match or contrast with the walls, and a painted interior in a darker tone can make shelves stand out. Solid oak or oak-veneer tops add warmth. We'll show you samples and sketches before anything is made. Handles, knobs and hinges are chosen last, once you've seen the doors, so the details suit the finished room.

How long it takes

Typically 1–3 weeks to make, 1–2 days to fit a pair

Questions about alcove units and shelving

How deep should alcove units be?

Low cupboards are usually made around 35 to 45 cm deep, depending on how deep the alcove is and what you want to store. Shelves above can be the same depth or slightly shallower. For a flush look, units can be set back to align with the face of the chimney breast, where the alcove is deep enough.

Can you build alcove units around a TV?

Yes. We can build units that house AV equipment in ventilated cupboards, with cable routes to the TV on the chimney breast or on a unit. An electrician can add sockets and data points before we fit. We'll plan the layout so cables are hidden and equipment has enough airflow.

Do alcove units need painting after fitting?

Usually a final coat is best applied once the units are fitted, filled and caulked to the walls, so the joints are invisible. We can supply units primed, ready for your decorator, or fully painted by us. Spray-finished doors can be finished in the workshop and fitted afterwards.
