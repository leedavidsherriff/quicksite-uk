A staircase that makes the hallway

New balustrades, spindles, handrails and treads, and creaky stairs quietened.

The staircase is often the first thing you see when you walk into a house, and a tired balustrade with boxed-in spindles or a wobbly handrail can make the whole hallway feel dated. Renovating a staircase is usually far quicker and cheaper than replacing it, and it's done in a few days without taking the stairs out of use for long. We fit new spindles, handrails and newel posts, oak and glass balustrades, and replacement treads, and we fix creaking stairs for homeowners in Pontypridd. We'll make sure any new balustrade meets current safety guidance.

Renovating an existing staircase

Most staircases are structurally sound, and only the balustrade and finishes need replacing. The old spindles and handrail are removed, and new newel posts are bolted securely to the strings. A new handrail and base rail are fitted, and spindles are cut and fixed at the correct spacing. Popular styles include square or chamfered white spindles with an oak handrail, turned Victorian-style spindles, and contemporary oak and glass. Worn treads can be capped with oak, and the risers painted for a crisp contrast. The work is usually done in two to four days. Handrails can be left natural, oiled or varnished, and newel caps come in flat, ball or pyramid styles to suit the house.

Safety rules for balustrades

Building regulations guidance for houses says balustrades on stairs and landings should normally be at least 900 mm high, and that gaps between spindles should not allow a 100 mm sphere to pass through. That's to prevent small children slipping through or getting their heads stuck. Horizontal rails that make the balustrade easy to climb should be avoided. Many older staircases don't meet these rules, and when replacing a balustrade it's sensible to bring it up to the current standard. Glass panels must be toughened or laminated safety glass, and fixings must take the load of someone leaning on them.

Fixing creaking stairs

Creaks usually come from treads and risers rubbing against each other or against the strings because the glue blocks and wedges underneath have come loose or shrunk. If the underside of the stairs is accessible, such as from a cupboard, we can re-glue and replace wedges and add new glue blocks, which is the best fix. If not, treads can be screwed down from above into the risers, with the screw heads hidden under the carpet or filled and painted. It's quick, relatively cheap and makes a big difference to a quiet house at night. Squeaks on landings are usually loose floorboards, which are screwed down to the joists.

How long it takes

Typically 2–4 days for a balustrade; half a day for creaking stairs

Questions about staircases and balustrades

Can I use my stairs during renovation?

Usually, yes, with care. We keep the stairs usable at the end of each day wherever possible, with temporary rails or protection if the balustrade has been removed. For some parts of the job, such as capping treads, there may be short periods where the stairs are best avoided, and we'll plan these with you.

How much does it cost to renovate a staircase?

Replacing the spindles and handrail on a straight staircase with a short landing usually costs a little over a thousand pounds in pine, and more for oak or glass. Winders, long landings, and capping treads all add to the cost. We'll give a written quote after measuring.

Do new balustrades need building regulations approval?

Like-for-like replacement of a balustrade doesn't usually need an application, but the new balustrade should meet current safety guidance on height and gaps. If the work is part of a larger project such as a loft conversion, building control will inspect it. We'll build to the current standard either way.
