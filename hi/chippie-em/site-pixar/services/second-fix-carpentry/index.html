Second fix carpentry is the finishing work that happens after plastering: skirting boards, architraves around doors, window boards, boxing in pipes, loft hatches and wall panelling. It's where you notice quality, because every mitre, scribe and joint is on show. We do second fix work for renovations, extensions and single rooms across Pontypridd, as well as smaller jobs like replacing a damaged run of skirting or boxing in a soil pipe. We work with all the common profiles and can match older mouldings, so repairs blend in with the rest of the house.

Skirting and architrave

Skirting protects the bottom of the wall from knocks and covers the gap between floor and plaster, while architrave frames doors and hides the joint between frame and wall. Both come in many profiles, from simple pencil round and chamfered to ogee and torus, and heights range from about 70 mm to 220 mm or more in period homes. External corners are mitred, and internal corners are best scribed, where one piece is cut to the profile of the other, so the joint stays tight as the timber moves. Boards are fixed with adhesive and pins or screws into the wall. Doors are checked for clearance before architrave goes on, so it all lines up.

Matching period mouldings

In older houses, original skirting and architrave often need patching after rewiring, damp works or knocking through walls. Many traditional profiles are still available, but some can only be matched by having a length of timber cut to the same profile by a joinery workshop. We can take a sample or profile and source a match. Joins between old and new sections are carefully scarfed and filled so they disappear once painted. Where the original is too damaged, replacing a whole wall in the matching profile is often the better option.

Boxing in, panelling and small jobs

Exposed pipes, soil stacks and cables can be boxed in with a neat timber frame and MDF or ply panels, with removable access panels where stopcocks or joints need to be reached. Loft hatches can be enlarged, insulated and fitted with new linings and hatch doors, or replaced with a loft ladder. Wall panelling, including shaker-style, board-and-batten, and tongue-and-groove, adds character and hides uneven walls. It's often combined with a dado or picture rail. We can also fit window boards, curtain battens and shelves as part of the same visit. Every panel and access hatch is fixed so it can be removed later without damaging the surrounding finish.

Questions about skirting, architrave and second fix

Should skirting go on before or after flooring?

It depends on the floor. With carpet, skirting normally goes on first, with a small gap underneath for the carpet to tuck under. With wood, laminate or tiles, fitting skirting after the floor hides the expansion gap without needing scotia. We'll plan the order with you and any flooring fitter.

Can you match my old skirting?

Usually, yes. Many period profiles are still made. If yours isn't available, we can take a sample to a joinery workshop and have timber cut to match. Joins between old and new are scarfed and filled, so once painted, it's hard to see where the repair is.

Is wall panelling a DIY job?

Simple panelling can be, but getting even spacing, clean joints and a flat finish on uneven walls takes time and practice. Panels need to be set out from a centre point so they fit evenly between corners, windows and sockets. We can also fit panelling ready for you to paint.
