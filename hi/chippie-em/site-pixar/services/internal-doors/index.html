Changing internal doors is one of the quickest ways to update a house, but hanging a door well is a skilled job. A new door has to be trimmed to a frame that's rarely square, the hinges cut in precisely, and the latch lined up so the door closes with a gentle click. We hang new internal doors, fit fire doors, replace frames and linings, and fix sticking or dropping doors for homeowners and landlords in Pontypridd. We can supply doors or fit ones you've bought, and we'll check the frames and floor levels before quoting.

Choosing new internal doors

Most internal doors in the UK are 1981 mm high and 686, 762 or 838 mm wide, but older houses often have non-standard sizes that need doors trimmed or made to measure. Hollow-core doors are light and cheap but feel flimsy. Solid-core and engineered doors feel substantial, reduce noise and hang better. Oak and oak-veneer doors can be supplied pre-finished or unfinished for oiling, while primed doors are ready for painting. Glazed doors let light through to hallways and must use safety glass. We'll advise on the style, weight and hardware that suit your house.

Hanging a door properly

A new door is trimmed to leave even gaps of around 2 to 3 mm at the top and sides, and a larger gap at the bottom to clear flooring, which matters if you're laying carpet or thicker floors later. Hinges are marked and cut in with a router or chisel so they sit flush, and heavy or solid doors get three hinges instead of two. The latch and strike plate are fitted so the door closes firmly without rattling, and handles are set at a comfortable height. Doors are sealed on all edges, including top and bottom, to stop them absorbing moisture. Once the door is hung, we check it swings freely without drifting open or closed on its own, which is a sign the frame is out of plumb.

Fire doors and regulations

In most two-storey houses, internal doors don't need to be fire doors. But in houses with a floor more than 4.5 metres above ground, such as a loft conversion, building regulations generally require a protected escape route, and doors onto the stairway usually need to be fire-resisting. Flats also have strict rules for the front door and doors onto escape routes. A fire door only works if it's fitted with the right frame gaps, fire-rated hinges and intumescent seals. We'll explain what's needed, and your building control officer can confirm.

How long it takes

Typically 1–1.5 hours per door; a whole house in 1–2 days

Questions about internal doors

How long does it take to hang an internal door?

Hanging a new door into an existing frame usually takes about an hour to an hour and a half, including fitting the hinges, latch and handles. Doors in older houses, or those needing more trimming, can take longer. A typical house of six to eight doors can often be done in a day or two.

Why does my door keep sticking?

Common causes are loose or worn hinges, a door that's swollen from damp, paint build-up on the edges, or the house settling so the frame is no longer square. Tightening or packing hinges often solves it. If the door has swollen, planing and resealing the edges usually works. We'll find the cause before taking wood off.

Do I need fire doors in my house?

Most two-storey houses don't require fire doors internally. If you're adding a loft room, creating a third storey, or converting to flats, building regulations usually require fire-resisting doors onto the escape route. Integral garage doors also have specific requirements. Check with building control or ask us to explain what's needed.
