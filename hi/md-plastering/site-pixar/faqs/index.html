Questions

Ask me anything.

The things people ask most, plus what the words on your quote actually mean.

Do you give free quotes?

Yes. We look at the walls and ceilings, check for hollow areas and damp, and send a written quote that breaks down preparation, plastering and waste removal.

How messy is plastering?

Skimming is fairly clean. Hacking off and taking down ceilings is dusty. We sheet floors, seal doorways and bag waste as we go, and clean up at the end of each day.

How do I pay?

For bigger jobs we may ask for a deposit to cover materials, with the rest on completion. Small jobs are paid when finished. You'll get an itemised invoice either way.

Are you insured?

Any plasterer working in your home should carry public liability insurance. Ask us for our insurance details and we'll send them before we start.

How long before I can decorate?

A skim usually needs three to seven days to dry in a warm room, and replastering or render longer. Always start with a mist coat of watered-down matt emulsion.

Do I need to empty the room?

It helps. For skimming we can work around wrapped furniture moved to the middle. For hacking off or ceiling work, the room should be emptied as far as possible.

Can you fix damp walls?

We can replaster after the cause is fixed. Plastering over damp without fixing leaks, gutters or bridged damp courses won't last, so we'll point out likely causes.

Do you remove the waste?

Yes. Rubble and old plaster are bagged and taken away, and the cost is shown in your quote. For big jobs we'll arrange a skip or waste collection.

Jargon buster

Words on your quote.

Skim
A thin finishing coat of plaster, usually two to three millimetres, over a sound surface.
Bonding
Either a backing plaster for building up walls, or the primer used to help plaster grip.
Dot and dab
Fixing plasterboard to masonry walls with blobs of adhesive, then skimming.
Scrim tape
Self-adhesive mesh tape bedded into plaster over joints and cracks to stop them reappearing.
Angle bead
A metal or plastic strip fixed to external corners to give a sharp, strong edge.
Bell-cast bead
A bead fitted at the bottom of render so it stops above the damp-proof course.
Mist coat
A watered-down first coat of emulsion that seals fresh plaster before decorating.
Artex
A textured coating common on ceilings; older types may contain asbestos.
Hacking off
Removing old plaster or render back to the brick or block beneath.
NHL
Natural hydraulic lime, graded 2, 3.5 or 5 by strength, used for lime render and mortar.
Drummy
Plaster that sounds hollow when tapped because it has come away from the wall.
