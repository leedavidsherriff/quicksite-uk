When the old plaster has had its day

Failed plaster hacked off back to brick, then rebuilt and skimmed to a flat finish.

Get my guide priceCall 07469 237714

When plaster is hollow across whole walls, crumbling, stained by old damp or has been patched so many times it's lumpy, a skim won't save it. Replastering means stripping back to the brick or block and building the wall up again. It's common in older terraces and semis during renovations, after a new damp-proof course, after rewiring, or when walls have been chased and patched. We replaster single walls, rooms and whole houses in Hengoed, choosing the backing system to suit the wall, and take the heavy rubble away so you don't have to deal with it.

Hacking off and assessing the wall

Old plaster is removed back to the masonry, along with any loose mortar. This is noisy, dusty work, so rooms are sealed with dust sheets and doors taped, and rubble is bagged as we go. Once the brickwork is exposed we can see what's really going on: failed lintels, old chimney openings, cracked bricks or wet patches that need sorting before anything is covered up. If we find something structural, or a damp problem that hasn't been fixed at source, we'll stop and show you. Plastering over a problem only hides it for a while. We also note the positions of any cables and pipes so they're protected before new plaster goes on.

Choosing the backing: render, bonding or board

There are three common ways to rebuild a wall. A sand and cement render scratch coat followed by a skim is tough, handles moisture well and is often specified after damp-proofing. A one-coat gypsum backing plaster such as hardwall or bonding is quicker and good for general repairs on dry walls. Dot and dab, where plasterboard is stuck to the masonry with adhesive and then skimmed, is fast, gives very flat walls and adds a little insulation, but it's not suitable where damp is present or for walls that need to hang heavy cupboards without special fixings. In older solid-wall houses, lime may be the better choice altogether.

Cost, disruption and drying times

Replastering costs several times more than a skim per square metre because of the extra labour, materials and waste. Expect a typical room to take three to five days, including hacking off, the backing coat and the finish. Drying takes longer too: backing coats need to cure before skimming, and a full replaster may need two to four weeks before decorating, particularly with sand and cement. Rewiring, new sockets and pipe runs should be finished before we start so that walls are only disturbed once. We'll put the order of trades into your quote.

Questions about replastering

How do I know if I need replastering or just a skim?

Tap the walls. If most of the surface sounds solid and only a few patches are hollow, a skim with patch repairs is usually fine. If large areas sound drummy, the plaster is crumbling or bulging, or there are old damp stains, replastering is normally needed. We'll check every wall and explain what we find before quoting.

Do I need to move out during replastering?

Not usually, but it's messy. We seal the working room off with sheeting and tape, bag waste as we go and clean up daily. Dust can still travel, so it's worth moving valuables and electronics out of nearby rooms. If you're doing several rooms, it's often easier to plan it around a holiday.

Should I replaster before or after rewiring?

After. The electrician needs to chase cables into the walls and fit back boxes before plastering, otherwise new plaster gets cut up and patched. The same goes for plumbing, new radiators and any wall-mounted fixings that need noggins. We're happy to work to a sequence with your other trades.
