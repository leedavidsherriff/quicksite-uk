Outside walls and woodwork that stand up to the weather

Masonry, render, fascias, windows and doors cleaned, repaired and weatherproofed.

The outside of a house takes a battering from wind-driven rain, frost and sun, and paint is its first line of defence. When it starts flaking, chalking or growing green, water gets behind it and the repair bill grows. We repaint rendered and pebble-dashed walls, masonry, fascias, soffits, bargeboards, timber windows, sills and front doors for homeowners in Caerphilly. The work is planned around the weather, because paint applied to damp walls or in cold, wet conditions fails early. Your quote sets out the preparation, access and coatings so you know exactly what protection you are paying for.

Preparing masonry and render

Every exterior starts with a clean. Walls are brushed or low-pressure washed to remove dirt, loose material and algae, then treated with a fungicidal wash and left to dry. Flaking paint is scraped back to a sound edge. Hairline cracks in render are filled with an exterior filler; wider cracks, blown render or failed pointing are pointed out to you before we paint, because paint will not hold a failing surface together. Chalky or powdery walls get a stabilising primer so the topcoat bonds. This part is slow and unglamorous, but it is the difference between a finish that lasts a couple of winters and one that lasts many years.

Masonry paint: smooth, textured or breathable

Smooth masonry paint suits most rendered walls and is easiest to keep clean. Fine-textured versions hide patchy render and small repairs better. On older solid-wall houses, and on lime render in particular, a breathable mineral or silicate paint lets moisture escape rather than trapping it, which helps prevent damp inside. Sealed acrylic coatings on those walls can cause more harm than good. Colour also matters: very dark colours absorb heat, fade faster and can show every patch repair. We'll recommend a coating based on the wall, its age and its exposure, not simply the cheapest tin.

Fascias, soffits, windows and doors

Timber is where exterior paint fails first. We sand back to sound paint, cut out small areas of soft or rotten wood and fill them with a two-part resin filler that moves with the timber, then prime bare wood before undercoat and topcoat. Putty on older windows is checked and renewed where cracked. Sills, the bottoms of doors and the lower rails of windows get extra attention because water sits there. For front doors we can use a high-gloss finish or a modern satin; both are available in weatherproof, microporous formulations that let timber breathe. uPVC can be painted with the right primer, but we'll explain the pros and cons first.

Access, weather and timing

Most two-storey houses can be reached safely with ladders and stand-offs, but chimney stacks, three-storey gables and awkward lean-tos may need a tower or scaffold, which we price upfront. Exterior work is best done from late spring to early autumn, when walls are dry and overnight temperatures stay above about five degrees. We check the forecast daily and will not paint onto damp surfaces just to hit a date. If a spell of rain interrupts the job, we protect what's done and come back as soon as the walls have dried out.

Questions about exterior painting

What is the best time of year to paint outside?

Late spring to early autumn is ideal. Masonry paint needs a dry wall and temperatures above about five degrees while it cures, with no rain for several hours after application. In the UK that usually means May to September, although dry spells either side can work. We watch the forecast and move dates rather than risk paint washing off.

How long does exterior masonry paint last?

A good-quality masonry paint on a properly prepared wall typically lasts many years before it needs redoing, although exposed, west-facing and coastal walls weather faster. Timber windows and fascias usually need attention sooner. The biggest factors are preparation and conditions on the day, which is why we don't cut corners on either.

Can you paint pebble-dash?

Yes. Pebble-dash takes more paint and more effort than smooth render because every stone has to be covered from several angles, so we roll it with a long-pile roller and brush in where needed. Loose stones and hollow patches are checked first. It's priced a little higher per square metre than smooth render to reflect the extra material and time.
