Woodwork that looks sharp and wipes clean

Doors, frames, skirting and staircases sanded, filled and repainted.

Yellowed gloss, chipped skirting and doors covered in years of drips make a freshly painted room look tired. Woodwork takes more time and skill than walls because every edge, panel and moulding is brushed by hand, and the finish shows every shortcut. We repaint internal doors, frames, architraves, skirting, window boards, stair spindles, handrails and panelling for homes in Caerphilly. We can freshen existing white gloss, switch you to a modern satin or eggshell, or take woodwork into a colour, which is a popular way to add character without touching the walls.

Gloss, satin or eggshell

Gloss is the hardest-wearing and the easiest to wipe, but it highlights every dent. Satin has a softer sheen, hides imperfections better and is the most popular choice today. Eggshell is lower sheen again and looks elegant, especially on panelling and in period homes, but it marks a little more easily. Water-based versions dry quickly, smell far less and stay white instead of yellowing, which makes them our usual recommendation indoors. Oil-based finishes flow out slightly smoother and are still useful for high-wear areas, so we'll talk through the trade-off with you.

Getting a smooth finish on old woodwork

Older woodwork usually carries many layers of paint, drips and brush marks. We sand everything to a smooth, dull surface, fill dents and open joints with a fine filler, and caulk the gap between skirting and wall so the line is crisp. Bare knots are sealed with knotting solution so resin does not bleed through the new paint. When moving from oil-based gloss to a water-based finish, a suitable primer or bonding undercoat is essential or the new paint can peel. Doors are painted in a set order, panels first and stiles last, to avoid lap marks. Hinges and handles are protected or removed. Window boards and sills get the same treatment, since they collect condensation and show wear quickly.

Staircases, spindles and banisters

Staircases are the most time-consuming woodwork in most houses. Each spindle has four faces and the joints at top and bottom collect dust and old drips. We sand and fill, then paint spindles, strings and newel posts, and can leave handrails in a natural varnished or oiled finish for contrast. If your stairs have a carpet runner we mask along it carefully, or we can work between carpet removal and fitting. Stripped pine or lead-painted woodwork in older houses needs specific precautions: we will test and discuss this with you before any sanding starts. On landings we set up a safe stair platform rather than balancing ladders on the treads, which protects both us and your carpet.

Questions about woodwork, doors and skirting

Can you paint water-based paint over oil-based gloss?

Yes, but not directly. Water-based paint won't bond to old oil gloss on its own and may peel or scratch off. The woodwork needs a thorough sand and a bonding primer or dedicated undercoat first. Once that's done, a water-based satin or gloss will stay white far longer than oil-based gloss, which tends to yellow indoors.

Why has my white gloss gone yellow?

Oil-based gloss naturally yellows over time, especially in rooms with little daylight or behind radiators. It's a chemical change in the paint rather than dirt, so cleaning won't fix it. Switching to a water-based system with the correct primer solves the problem, as modern acrylic finishes keep their colour far better.

Should I paint the doors a different colour from the walls?

It's increasingly popular. Painting doors and woodwork in a deeper shade of the wall colour, or the same colour in a harder-wearing sheen, can make a room feel more considered. White woodwork remains a safe choice for resale. We're happy to paint a test panel so you can see a colour in your own light.
