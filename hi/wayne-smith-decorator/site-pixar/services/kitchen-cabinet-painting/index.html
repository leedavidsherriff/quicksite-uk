A new-look kitchen without ripping it out

Cabinet doors, drawers and frames degreased, primed and refinished in a durable colour.

If your kitchen units are solid and the layout works, painting them can transform the room for a fraction of the cost and disruption of a replacement. The worktops stay, the plumbing stays, and you keep using the kitchen for most of the job. We paint timber, MDF, veneered and many foil and laminate doors for homeowners in Aberdare, using primers and finishes designed to cope with steam, grease and daily knocks. You choose the colour, handles and whether to include the plinths, end panels and island, and we'll price each part clearly.

Why kitchen doors need a different approach

Kitchen doors are handled dozens of times a day and coated in a film of cooking grease you often can't see. Paint over that and it will peel within weeks. Every surface is degreased with a dedicated cleaner, rinsed and dried, then keyed with fine abrasive. Glossy laminates and foils get a specialist adhesion primer, and a test patch is done first because some foil-wrapped doors are too far gone to hold paint. Finishes are hard-wearing, often waterborne, formulations built for furniture and cabinets, applied by brush and fine roller or sprayed, to give a smooth, factory-style surface. Glass-fronted and open shelving units are masked carefully so only the frames are coated.

Sprayed or hand-painted finish

Spraying gives the smoothest, most even result and is ideal for flat slab doors. Doors and drawer fronts are usually taken away, numbered and sprayed in a clean, dust-controlled space, then returned once cured, while the carcass fronts and end panels are brushed and rolled on site. A skilled hand-painted finish using a fine foam or microfibre roller also looks excellent, particularly on shaker and in-frame styles, and keeps the whole job in your home. Both are durable; the choice usually comes down to door style, budget and how long you can be without doors.

Colours, handles and small upgrades

Painting is the ideal moment to change handles. New handles that use the same hole centres avoid filling; changing the size means holes are filled, sanded and redrilled. Popular colour schemes pair a darker base run with lighter wall units, or paint only an island in a strong colour for contrast. Plinths can be painted to match or replaced. Worn hinges can be swapped for soft-close ones for a modest extra cost. Inside the cabinets is not normally painted, as it takes heavy wear from pans and crockery and is rarely visible. Before starting we paint a sample door so you can see the colour against your worktop, floor and splashback in your own light.

How long it takes

Typically 3–5 days on site; sprayed doors back within a week

Questions about kitchen cabinet painting

Can you paint laminate or foil-wrapped kitchen doors?

Often, yes, as long as the surface is firmly bonded. We clean and key the doors and apply a specialist adhesion primer, then do a test patch to check the bond before committing. If a foil is peeling or bubbling at the edges it will keep lifting, and in that case new doors are a better investment.

How long will painted kitchen cabinets last?

With proper degreasing, the right primer and a cabinet-grade topcoat, a painted kitchen should last for many years of normal use. The areas that wear first are around handles and the doors under the sink. Leftover paint is left with you so small chips can be touched in, and a door can be refreshed individually if needed.

Can I use my kitchen while it's being painted?

Mostly, yes. The sink, hob and fridge stay in use, though some cupboards will be empty or doorless for a few days. Water-based finishes keep smells low. Paint takes a couple of weeks to reach full hardness, so we'll ask you to treat doors gently at first and avoid harsh cleaners during that time.
