Most rooms don't need a new colour so much as a proper job: cracks filled so they don't come back, old marks sealed so they don't bleed through, and a crisp line where the wall meets the ceiling. We paint single rooms, whole floors and full houses in Merthyr Tydfil, working around families, pets and people working from home. Every quote is priced room by room, so you can see exactly what walls, ceilings and woodwork cost, and choose to do the lot or phase it. Furniture is moved and covered, floors are sheeted, and the room is usable again each evening where possible.

Why preparation is most of the job

On a typical repaint, preparation takes as long as the painting itself. Hairline cracks around ceilings and door frames are raked out and filled with a flexible filler, not just skimmed over, or they reappear within a season. Nail holes and dents are filled twice and sanded flush. Glossy or previously sealed surfaces get a key so the new paint grips. Water stains, nicotine, crayon and smoke marks are spot-primed with a stain-blocking primer, because emulsion alone will let them ghost back through. Where there is new plaster we apply a diluted mist coat first. Skipping any of this is how cheap jobs look tired within a year.

Choosing the right paint for each room

Standard matt emulsion looks lovely but marks easily, so for hallways, stairs and children's rooms we usually recommend a durable or scrubbable matt, which you can wipe without leaving a shiny patch. Kitchens and bathrooms benefit from a paint formulated for humidity, which resists mould and steam far better than ordinary emulsion. Ceilings are best in a flat matt that hides imperfections. Deep, strong colours often need a third coat and a tinted undercoat to reach full depth, which we price in advance rather than add later. If you have a trade or retail brand in mind we'll use it; if not, we'll recommend one that suits the room and budget.

What affects the price of a room

Size matters less than people think. What moves the price is the condition of the walls, the number of doors, windows and radiators to cut around, ceiling height, and whether woodwork is included. A stairwell costs more than a bedroom of the same floor area because it needs a platform or tower to reach safely. A big colour change, such as dark to light or white to navy, adds a coat. Heavily damaged walls may be better skimmed first, and we'll tell you honestly if painting over them won't give the finish you want. Our quote lists walls, ceiling and woodwork separately so nothing is hidden.

Living with the job while we work

We move furniture to the middle of the room and cover it with clean dust sheets, protect carpets and floors, and take down curtains, pictures and light switch covers where it's safe to do so. Low-odour water-based paints mean most rooms can be slept in the same night. We clean brushes off site or in a sink you're happy for us to use, and we hoover and wipe down before we leave each day. At the end we put everything back and walk round the room with you, so any touch-up is done while we're still there.

How long it takes

Questions about interior painting

How long does it take to paint a room?

An average bedroom with walls and ceiling usually takes one full day, or two if woodwork is included. A living room with more filling or a strong colour change can take two days. Hallways, stairs and landings take longest because of the access equipment and the number of doors, and usually run to two or three days.

Do I need to buy the paint?

You can, but most customers let us supply it. We buy at trade rates, know how much each room needs and choose the right sheen for each surface. If you already have a brand and colour picked, just give us the name and code and we'll price it in. Leftover paint is labelled and left with you for touch-ups.

Can I stay in the house while you paint?

Yes. We use low-odour water-based paints for walls and ceilings, so rooms are usually fine to use by the evening. We keep one room clear and usable where possible and work around school runs and home working. Oil-based gloss on woodwork smells more, so if that's included we'll discuss ventilation and timing with you.
