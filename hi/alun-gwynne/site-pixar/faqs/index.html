Questions

Ask me anything.

The things people ask most, plus what the words on your quote actually mean.

Do you offer free quotes?

Yes. We visit, look at the surfaces and talk through what you want, then send a written quote that lists each room or area separately. For small jobs we can often quote from photos and measurements.

How do I pay?

For larger jobs we may ask for a deposit to cover materials, with the balance due on completion once you're happy. Smaller jobs are paid on completion. Bank transfer is preferred, and you'll always get an itemised invoice.

Are you insured?

Any decorator working in your home should carry public liability insurance. Ask us for our insurance details and we'll send them before we start.

Will you protect my furniture and floors?

Yes. Furniture is moved and covered with clean dust sheets, carpets and floors are sheeted and edges are masked. We tidy at the end of each day and clean thoroughly when we finish.

What happens if I'm not happy with an area?

Tell us during the final walk-round and we'll put it right before we leave. If something shows up after we've gone, such as a missed patch in a different light, call us and we'll come back.

Do I need to move out while you paint?

No. We use low-odour water-based paints for most interior work and plan the job so you always have somewhere to sit, sleep and cook. We'll talk through any rooms you need clear on particular days.

What about older houses and lead paint?

Houses built before the 1960s can have lead paint on woodwork. If we suspect it, we'll test before sanding and use methods that avoid creating dust. Lead dust is a real hazard for children and pregnant women, so we take it seriously.

How far ahead do you book up?

It varies with the season. Exterior work books up for spring and summer, so it pays to ask early. Interior jobs can usually be scheduled sooner. Get in touch and we'll give you an honest start date.

Jargon buster

Words on your quote.

Cutting in
Painting a sharp line by brush where two surfaces or colours meet, such as wall and ceiling.
Mist coat
A watered-down first coat of emulsion that seals new, porous plaster so later coats don't peel.
Stain block
A primer that stops water marks, smoke and nicotine bleeding through new paint.
Sheen
How shiny a paint dries, from flat matt through eggshell and satin to gloss.
Caulk
A flexible filler run along joints, such as between skirting and wall, to give a clean line.
Keying
Lightly sanding a shiny surface so new paint has something to grip.
Knotting
A sealer painted over knots in bare timber to stop resin bleeding through.
Lining paper
Plain paper hung before wallpaper or paint to create a smooth, even base.
Pattern repeat
The distance before a wallpaper design repeats, which affects how many rolls you need.
Microporous
An exterior finish that lets moisture escape from timber while keeping rain out.
