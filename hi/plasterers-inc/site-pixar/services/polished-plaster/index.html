Polished plaster gives a wall a depth and movement that paint can't match. Built from thin layers of lime and marble powder, or modern acrylic equivalents, and burnished by hand, it can look like polished marble, soft suede or aged stone. It's popular for feature walls, chimney breasts, hallways, bathrooms and commercial reception areas. If you're considering it for a home or business in Doncaster, we can bring sample boards in different colours and sheens, talk through where it works best, and price the prep and finish separately so the cost is clear.

Venetian, marmorino and other finishes

Traditional Venetian plaster, or stucco veneziano, is lime putty with fine marble dust, applied in several very thin coats and burnished to a high, glassy sheen with natural variation. Marmorino uses a coarser marble grain and gives a softer, more matt, stone-like finish. Tadelakt, a Moroccan lime plaster polished with stones and sealed with soap, is naturally water-resistant and is used in showers and wet rooms. There are also acrylic polished plasters, which are more flexible and consistent in colour, but less breathable and with a slightly different depth. Each finish can be tinted to almost any colour.

Preparation makes or breaks it

Polished plaster is only a millimetre or two thick, so the wall beneath has to be flat, sound and dry. Most walls need a fresh skim first, or a new board, and must be fully dried before the finish goes on. A specialist primer is then applied, sometimes with a fine sand, to give the first coat something to key into. Sockets and switches are removed, corners are formed carefully and adjoining surfaces masked. Rushing the base coats, or applying over damp plaster, leads to cracking and dull patches. That's why we price the preparation and finish separately.

Cost, care and where to use it

Because every coat is hand-applied and burnished, polished plaster costs many times more per square metre than a skim, and a feature wall is often the best-value way to introduce it. Lime-based finishes are breathable, naturally resistant to mould and develop character with age, but can be marked by oils and harsh cleaners. A protective wax or sealer is applied at the end for kitchens, bathrooms and high-traffic areas. Small chips can be locally repaired and reburnished. It pairs well with simple, modern interiors and with period homes, where it can echo original lime surfaces. We leave you written care notes and a small amount of the tinted material for future repairs.

Questions about polished and venetian plaster

Is polished plaster suitable for bathrooms?

Yes, with the right product and sealer. Lime-based Venetian plaster sealed with wax works well on bathroom walls away from direct spray. For showers and wet rooms, tadelakt or a specialist waterproof polished system is the better choice. Good ventilation is still important, as with any bathroom finish.

How durable is polished plaster?

Very, once cured and sealed. Lime-based plasters harden further over time. The surface can be scratched by sharp objects and marked by oils, but small damage can usually be repaired and reburnished locally without redoing the wall. It's considerably tougher than most paint finishes.

Can polished plaster go over my existing walls?

Only if they're flat, sound and dry. Most walls need a fresh skim first, which then has to dry fully. Painted walls may need a special primer. We'll check your walls and include any preparation in the quote, as the finish will show every lump underneath.
