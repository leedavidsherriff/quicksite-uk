Ceilings take more punishment than walls. Old lath and plaster sags, artex swirls date a room, and cracks appear along every board joint when a house moves. Whether you want to lose a textured ceiling, fix a sagging one or put up a new ceiling after a leak, we can make it flat and smooth again. We work across Doncaster on bedrooms, living rooms, kitchens and hallways, and give clear advice on the safest way to deal with artex. That means testing where needed and choosing a method that avoids disturbing it wherever possible.

Artex and asbestos: what you should know

Textured coatings applied before the mid-1980s may contain a small amount of white asbestos. Undisturbed and painted, it's low risk, but sanding, scraping or drilling it can release fibres. If your artex could be that old, we recommend a sample is tested by an accredited laboratory before work begins, which is quick and inexpensive. If it comes back clear, it can be scraped or skimmed without special measures. If it contains asbestos, the usual approach is to leave it in place and cover it, either with a skim over a bonding coat or by overboarding. Removal of asbestos artex should only be carried out by an appropriately trained contractor.

Skim over or overboard?

A lightly stippled artex can usually be sealed and skimmed in two or three coats. A heavy swirl or peak pattern may need knocking back, if it's asbestos-free, or a bonding coat to fill the pattern before skimming. Overboarding means screwing new plasterboard through the existing ceiling into the joists, taping the joints and skimming. It's often the cleanest and most reliable option for heavily textured or cracked ceilings, and it avoids disturbing what's above. It adds a small amount of weight and lowers the ceiling by around a centimetre, which is rarely noticeable. Coving, if you want it, is fitted after skimming and before decorating.

Replacing a failed ceiling

If a ceiling is sagging, badly water-damaged or the old lath and plaster is coming away, the only lasting fix is to take it down and board it again. It's a dusty job, so the room is emptied and sealed. Joists are checked and any damaged timber is flagged. We fix new plasterboard at the correct spacing with screws, stagger the joints, tape and skim, and can fit moisture-resistant board in kitchens and bathrooms. If you'd like better sound insulation between floors, acoustic board or mineral wool between the joists can be added while the ceiling is open.

How long it takes

Typically 1–2 days per ceiling; up to 3 days to take down and reboard

Questions about ceilings, boarding and artex

Does my artex contain asbestos?

You can't tell by looking. Textured coatings applied before the mid-1980s may contain asbestos, and later ones generally don't. The only way to know is a lab test on a small sample. It's inexpensive and gives peace of mind. If it's positive, covering it by skimming or overboarding is usually the safest and simplest solution.

Is it better to remove artex or cover it?

Covering it is usually quicker, cleaner and cheaper. Scraping artex off is slow, messy and can damage the ceiling underneath. If the artex contains asbestos, covering avoids disturbing it. Removal only makes sense when the ceiling is coming down anyway, or when the artex is clear and fairly thin.

Can a lath and plaster ceiling be saved?

Sometimes. If it's mostly sound and only cracked, it can often be overboarded or repaired with lime. If it's sagging, bouncing when pressed or has come away from the laths, it needs to come down. We'll check it carefully, as a failing lath and plaster ceiling can fall without much warning.
