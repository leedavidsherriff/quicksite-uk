Questions

Ask me anything.

The things people ask most, plus what the words on your quote actually mean.

Do you charge by the hour or by the job?

Most jobs are priced per job so you know the cost upfront. Longer lists can be booked as a half or full day, and we'll get through as many as we can.

Is there a minimum charge?

Yes, there's a minimum charge that covers travel and the first hour. Adding other small jobs to the same visit makes it better value.

Do you bring materials?

We bring tools and standard fixings. Larger items like shelves, TV brackets or fence panels are supplied by you, or we can pick them up and add the cost.

Are you insured?

Any handyperson working in your home should carry public liability insurance. Ask us for our insurance details and we'll send them before we start.

Do you do electrical or gas work?

No. Gas work must only be done by a registered gas engineer, and new electrical circuits by a registered electrician. We'll tell you when a job needs one.

How do I pay?

Payment is on completion, by bank transfer or card. For larger jobs with materials, we may ask for a deposit. You'll get a written invoice.

What should I have ready?

A list of jobs, any items to be fitted, and clear access to the areas we're working in. Photos in advance help us price accurately and bring the right parts.

Can you help landlords and letting agents?

Yes. We do small repairs between and during tenancies, can collect keys from agents, and send photos and an itemised invoice after each job.

Jargon buster

Words on your quote.

Cavity fixing
A wall anchor that grips the back of plasterboard, used where there's no stud to screw into.
Stud
A vertical timber or metal upright inside a plasterboard wall, strong enough for heavy fixings.
Anti-tip strap
A strap or bracket fixing tall furniture to the wall so it can't fall forward.
VESA pattern
The standard spacing of mounting holes on the back of a TV, which the bracket must match.
Isolation valve
A small valve on the pipe to a tap or toilet that shuts off water for repairs.
Strike plate
The metal plate on the door frame that the latch clicks into.
Euro cylinder
A common type of lock barrel on uPVC and composite doors.
Gravel board
A board at the base of a fence that keeps panels off the ground to stop rot.
Postcrete
A fast-setting concrete mix used to set fence posts in the ground.
Stain block
A primer that stops water and smoke stains bleeding through fresh paint.
