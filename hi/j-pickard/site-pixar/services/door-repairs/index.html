A door that sticks, won't latch or rattles in the frame is one of those jobs that's easy to live with and annoying every day. Most door problems have a simple cause, such as loose hinges, a swollen edge or a strike plate that no longer lines up, and are quick to fix once you know where to look. We repair and adjust internal doors, and many external timber doors, for homes and rentals in Trowbridge, and we also replace handles, latches, hinges and cylinders. If a door is past saving, we can hang a new one in the same visit.

Why doors stick, drop and rattle

The most common cause of a sticking door is loose or worn hinges, which let the door drop and catch on the frame at the top or bottom corner. Tightening the screws, or replacing stripped holes with longer screws or timber plugs, often solves it. Doors can also swell in damp weather, especially if their top and bottom edges were never painted or sealed. Paint build-up on edges and frames is another cause. A door that won't latch usually has a strike plate out of line with the latch, and a door that rattles needs its stop or strike plate adjusting so it closes firmly.

Easing, planing and rehanging

Once hinges are secure, we look at where the door is rubbing, often marked by worn paint. Small amounts are planed off the edge, then the bare timber is sealed or painted so it doesn't absorb moisture and swell again. If a door has dropped, hinges can be packed or their positions adjusted to lift it. Doors that catch on new carpets or flooring can be taken off and trimmed at the bottom. Where the frame itself has moved out of square, the stops may need moving. We'll always try the simplest fix first before taking wood off.

Handles, latches, locks and draughts

Worn lever handles, loose knobs and sticking latches are quick to replace, and new handles are an easy way to update a house. Tubular latches, hinges and bathroom privacy locks are like-for-like swaps. On external doors, we can replace worn euro cylinders with anti-snap cylinders, which resist a common method of break-in, and fit door chains, viewers and letterbox draught excluders. Draughty doors can be improved with brush or compression seals around the frame and a threshold seal at the bottom. For uPVC and composite door mechanisms, we'll tell you honestly if it's a job for a specialist.

Questions about door repairs and adjustments

Why does my door stick in winter but not summer?

Timber doors absorb moisture in damp weather and swell slightly, then shrink when it's dry. If the top and bottom edges were never painted, they soak up more moisture. Planing the tight spot in winter and sealing all the edges usually solves the problem for good, as long as the hinges are also secure.

Can you fix uPVC doors?

We can adjust hinges and replace handles and cylinders on many uPVC and composite doors. Problems with the multipoint locking mechanism inside the door often need a specialist door or locksmith engineer with the right parts. We'll look at it and let you know honestly whether it's something we can do.

Is it worth fitting an anti-snap cylinder?

For many external uPVC and composite doors, yes. Standard euro cylinders can be vulnerable to lock-snapping, a common burglary method. An anti-snap cylinder is designed to resist this. It's a quick swap, but the cylinder must be the correct size so it doesn't stick out from the handle.
