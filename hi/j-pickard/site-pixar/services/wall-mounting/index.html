Putting things on walls sounds simple until the plug spins in a crumbling hole, the drill hits a pipe, or the shelf sags under a row of books. The secret is choosing the right fixing for the wall and knowing what's hidden behind it. We put up TVs, shelves, mirrors, pictures, curtain poles, blinds, coat hooks and bathroom accessories for homes in Trowbridge, on brick, block, plasterboard, lath and plaster and tiles. Everything is level, secure and in the right place, with a detector used to check for cables and pipes before any drilling.

Knowing your wall

Most UK houses have a mix of wall types. Solid brick and block walls take standard plugs and screws well, but older soft bricks and mortar joints can crumble, so plugs are placed carefully. Plasterboard walls are hollow, so heavy items need screws into the timber or metal studs behind, or heavy-duty cavity fixings such as spring toggles or metal cavity anchors. Lath and plaster in older houses can be fragile and needs gentle drilling. Tiled walls need a tile bit and slow drilling to avoid cracking. Cables usually run vertically or horizontally from sockets and switches, which is why we check with a detector first.

Mounting a TV safely

A TV bracket must match both the TV's VESA mounting pattern and its weight, and the wall must be strong enough to carry it, especially with a tilting or full-motion arm that puts extra leverage on the fixings. On masonry walls we use heavy-duty plugs and coach screws; on plasterboard we fix into studs or use fixings rated for the load. The centre of the screen is usually set around eye height when seated. Cables can be run in a surface trunking painted to match the wall. Running cables inside a wall, or adding a new socket behind the TV, is a job for an electrician.

Shelves, curtain poles and blinds

Shelves need brackets spaced to suit the load; long shelves full of books can sag between brackets, so we'll suggest a thicker shelf or extra support if needed. Floating shelves need a solid wall or strong stud fixing, as all the weight is on the hidden bracket. Curtain poles are usually fitted 10 to 15 cm above the window and wider than the frame, so curtains can stack back and let in more light. Heavy curtains need a central bracket on longer poles. Blinds can be fitted inside the window recess or on the face of the wall, and we'll check the recess depth and any handles in the way.

How long it takes

Typically 1 hour for a TV; most shelves and poles 20–40 minutes each

Questions about tvs, shelves and wall mounting

Can a TV be mounted on a plasterboard wall?

Yes, in most cases. The safest method is to fix the bracket into the timber or metal studs behind the plasterboard. If the studs aren't where they need to be, heavy-duty cavity fixings rated for the load can be used for smaller TVs. Full-motion arms and large TVs need extra care because of the leverage.

Can you hide the TV cables in the wall?

Running cables inside a wall and adding a socket behind the TV should be done by an electrician. What we can do is fit neat surface trunking from the TV down to the socket, which can be painted to match the wall so it's barely noticeable. It's a quicker and cheaper option.

What if I drill into a pipe or cable?

That's why we always check with a detector and avoid the zones where cables usually run, vertically and horizontally from sockets and switches. Detectors aren't perfect, so we also consider where pipes and cables are likely to be, such as above radiators and around light switches, before drilling.
