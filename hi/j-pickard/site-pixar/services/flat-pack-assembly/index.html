Flat-pack built square, solid and safely fixed

Wardrobes, beds, drawers and desks built, levelled and anchored to the wall.

Flat-pack furniture is cheap to buy but expensive in time and patience to build, especially big wardrobes that need two people and a wall fixing. Built badly, it wobbles, doors hang crooked and drawers stick. We assemble flat-pack furniture from all the major retailers for homes, flats and offices in Trowbridge: wardrobes, chests of drawers, beds, desks, bookcases, sideboards, kitchen islands and garden furniture. Everything is built square, levelled on uneven floors, adjusted so doors and drawers line up, and fixed to the wall where it should be. Packaging can be taken away too.

Why wall fixing matters

Tall wardrobes, bookcases and chests of drawers can tip forward if a child climbs them or pulls out several drawers at once, and furniture tip-over causes serious injuries every year. Almost all tall flat-pack furniture comes with an anti-tip strap or bracket for this reason, and it should always be fitted. The right fixing depends on the wall: wall plugs and screws for brick and block, cavity fixings or screws into timber studs for plasterboard. We check for pipes and cables with a detector before drilling. If a piece is going somewhere it can't be fixed, we'll discuss safer options.

Getting it right on uneven floors

Very few floors are perfectly level, especially in older houses and on carpets, and a wardrobe that's slightly out of level will have doors that won't close or that drift open. We level larger pieces with adjustable feet or packers under the base before fixing, check that the carcass is square by measuring the diagonals, and then hang and adjust the doors using the hinge screws until the gaps are even. Sliding wardrobe doors are adjusted so they run smoothly. Drawer runners are aligned so drawers close fully and line up with each other.

Big pieces, systems and kitchens

Large wardrobe systems, fitted-look storage and wall-to-wall combinations take longer and often need two people, especially where tall frames have to be stood up under a low ceiling. We'll ask for the product names and sizes when you book so we can price the job accurately and bring the right help. Flat-pack kitchen units can be assembled, levelled and fixed together, although connecting sinks and hobs needs a plumber and electrician, and gas appliances must only be fitted by a registered gas engineer. Garden sheds, storage boxes and play equipment can also be built on a suitable level base.

Booking and what to have ready

Before the visit, it helps to check that all the boxes have arrived and are in the room where the furniture will go, as carrying a built wardrobe up the stairs is rarely possible. Clear a working space around where it will stand. We bring the tools and fixings needed, and we'll let you know if a missing part turns up before we get too far. Packaging can be flattened and left for your recycling, or taken away for a small charge.

Questions about flat-pack assembly

How long does it take to build a flat-pack wardrobe?

A standard double wardrobe usually takes around one and a half to two hours, including levelling, adjusting the doors and fixing it to the wall. Larger wardrobe systems with sliding doors or internal fittings can take three to five hours. Chests of drawers and beds are usually around an hour each.

Do you take the packaging away?

We can. We'll flatten all the cardboard and bag the plastic, then either leave it for your recycling collection or take it away for a small charge, which we'll show in the quote. Larger jobs with a lot of packaging may need a separate waste trip, and we'll let you know beforehand.

Can you move or rebuild furniture?

Yes. Flat-pack furniture can often be taken apart and rebuilt in another room or a new home, although chipboard can weaken if taken apart several times. We'll check the joints and fixings and replace worn cam locks or dowels if needed. Heavy or awkward pieces may need two people.
