Asset finance, explained

Asset finance spreads the cost of vans, machinery and equipment over time. The item you’re buying is the security, which often makes it easier and cheaper to arrange than an unsecured loan.

The main types

  • Hire purchase: you pay a deposit, then monthly instalments, and you own the asset at the end. Good if you want to keep it for its whole working life.
  • Finance lease: you rent the asset for most of its life and don’t own it outright. Payments can be lower, and there are options at the end of the term.
  • Operating lease or contract hire: you use the asset for a fixed period and hand it back. Common for vans and equipment you replace regularly.
  • Refinancing: raising cash against equipment you already own.

Deposits and balloons

Many agreements ask for a deposit. Some include a larger final ‘balloon’ payment to keep the monthly cost down. That can help cash flow, but you need to plan for the balloon when it arrives.

What lenders look at

The value and type of the asset matter as much as your accounts. Lenders are generally happier with items that hold their value, like vans and well-known machinery. They’ll still look at your trading history, credit file and affordability.

Tax and VAT

Hire purchase and leases are treated differently for tax and VAT. Your accountant can tell you which suits your business. We can explain how each works, but we don’t give tax advice.

Questions people ask

Can I finance a used van?

Often, yes. Many lenders fund used vehicles and equipment, though age and mileage limits vary.

Do I need a deposit?

Not always, but a deposit usually lowers the monthly cost and can improve your chances of acceptance.

I’m a new business. Can I still get asset finance?

Sometimes. Because the asset secures the finance, some lenders will consider newer businesses, often with a larger deposit.

I’m a sole trader. Is anything different?

If you’re a sole trader or a small partnership borrowing £25,000 or less, the agreement is usually regulated under the Consumer Credit Act, which gives you extra protections.
