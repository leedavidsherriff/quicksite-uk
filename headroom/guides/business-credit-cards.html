Business credit cards, explained

A business credit card gives you a spending limit for day-to-day costs, a buffer for surprises, and clean statements your accountant will thank you for.

How it works

You spend up to your limit and get a monthly statement. If you clear the balance in full each month, many cards charge no interest on purchases. If you don’t, interest applies to what’s left.

When a card beats a loan

For smaller, repeating costs like fuel, materials, software and travel, a card is simpler than borrowing a lump sum. For a large one-off purchase, a loan or asset finance is usually cheaper.

Things to check

  • Any annual or monthly fee.
  • The interest rate if you carry a balance.
  • Whether directors are personally liable.
  • Cards for employees, and spending controls.
  • Cashback or rewards. Nice, but never the main reason to choose.

Questions people ask

Will it affect my personal credit?

Applications usually involve a credit search, and some cards need a personal guarantee. We’ll explain before you apply.

Can a new business get one?

Often, yes. Limits for new businesses tend to start lower and grow over time.
