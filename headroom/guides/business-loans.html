Business loans, explained

A business loan is a lump sum you repay in fixed monthly instalments over an agreed term. It’s the all-rounder: good for growth plans, one-off costs and bridging a cash gap.

How a business loan works

You borrow a set amount, and the lender agrees a term and a monthly repayment up front. You know exactly what goes out each month and when the loan ends, which makes it easy to plan around.

The cost is usually shown as interest plus any arrangement fee. Before you sign, you should see the total amount repayable, so you can compare options on what they really cost rather than on the monthly figure alone.

Unsecured or secured

  • Unsecured: no asset is pledged. Decisions tend to be quicker, but amounts are usually lower and the cost higher. Lenders to limited companies often ask directors for a personal guarantee.
  • Secured: backed by property or other assets. It can mean larger amounts and a lower rate, but the asset is at risk if repayments aren’t kept up.

What lenders look at

  • How long you’ve been trading. Most lenders want at least 6 to 12 months, and some want two years.
  • Turnover and how steady it is, usually from recent business bank statements.
  • Your business and personal credit history.
  • Existing debts and what you already repay each month.
  • What the money is for, and whether the repayments are affordable.

When a loan isn’t the best fit

If you’re buying a van or machinery, asset finance is often cheaper because the item itself secures it. If cash is stuck in unpaid invoices, invoice finance can release it without adding a monthly loan repayment. If most of your takings come in by card, a merchant cash advance flexes with your sales. Part of our job is telling you when a loan isn’t the right tool.

Questions people ask

Will a business loan affect my credit score?

Our questions don’t involve a credit search. If you apply to a lender, they will usually search your credit file, and we’ll tell you before that happens.

What is a personal guarantee?

A promise from a director that they will repay the loan personally if the company can’t. Many unsecured loans to limited companies ask for one, so it’s worth understanding before you apply.

Can I repay early?

Often, yes, but some lenders charge an early repayment fee. It’s one of the things to check when comparing offers.

I’m a sole trader. Is anything different?

If you’re a sole trader or a small partnership borrowing £25,000 or less, the loan is usually regulated under the Consumer Credit Act, which gives you extra protections. We’ll handle it under the rules that apply.
