Invoice finance, explained

Invoice finance lets you borrow against invoices your customers haven’t paid yet. Instead of waiting 30, 60 or 90 days, you get most of the money now.

How it works

You raise an invoice as normal. The finance provider advances a large share of its value, often within a day or two. When your customer pays, you receive the rest, minus the provider’s fees.

Because the facility is tied to your sales ledger, it grows as you invoice more. That makes it useful for businesses that are growing quickly and keep running short of cash despite being busy.

Factoring or discounting

  • Invoice factoring: the provider runs your credit control and chases payment. Your customers will know you use it. It suits smaller businesses without someone chasing invoices.
  • Invoice discounting: you keep chasing your own customers, and it’s usually confidential. It tends to suit larger businesses with good credit control.

What it costs

There’s usually a service fee (often a percentage of turnover or of each invoice) and a discount charge on the money you’ve drawn, similar to interest. Some facilities have minimum fees or notice periods, so it’s worth checking the full terms, not just the headline percentage.

Who it suits

Builders and trades working for main contractors, manufacturers, wholesalers, recruitment agencies, haulage firms and anyone else whose customers are other businesses paying on terms. It doesn’t help businesses that are paid on the spot by the public.

Questions people ask

Will my customers know?

With factoring, yes, because the provider collects payment. Invoice discounting is usually confidential.

What if a customer doesn’t pay?

That depends on whether the facility is recourse or non-recourse. With recourse, you’d have to cover the unpaid amount. Non-recourse includes bad-debt protection, at a higher cost.

Can I finance just one invoice?

Yes. Selective or single-invoice finance lets you pick individual invoices rather than signing up your whole ledger.

Do I need to have traded for years?

Not always. Because the lending is based on your invoices and customers, newer businesses with good customers can sometimes qualify.
