Development finance, explained

Development finance funds building, converting or heavily refurbishing property. The money is released in stages as the work goes up, rather than as one lump sum.

How it works

The lender usually funds part of the land or property purchase and most of the build cost. After each stage is finished, a surveyor checks the work and the lender releases the next payment. Interest is normally rolled up and repaid when the units are sold or refinanced.

The numbers lenders use

  • Loan to cost: how much of the total project cost they’ll fund.
  • Loan to GDV: how much the loan is compared with the gross development value, meaning what the finished project should be worth.
  • Your contribution: the cash you put in yourself.

Experience matters

Lenders like to see a track record, or an experienced team around you: builder, architect and project manager. First-time developers can still get funding, but usually on smaller projects or with more of their own money in.

What you’ll need

Planning permission, costed build plans, a schedule of works, the expected end values, and a clear plan for selling or refinancing at the end.

Questions people ask

Can I get development finance without planning permission?

Most development lenders want planning in place. Before that, some buyers use bridging finance to secure the site.

Is it only for big developers?

No. Plenty of lenders fund small schemes, like one to four houses or a single conversion.

What if the build overruns?

Build in a contingency. Lenders will usually expect one in your costings.
