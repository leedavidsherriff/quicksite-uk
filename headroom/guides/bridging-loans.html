Bridging loans, explained

A bridging loan is short-term finance secured on property. It ‘bridges’ a gap: buying before a sale completes, securing a property at auction, or funding a refurb before refinancing.

How it works

The lender advances a percentage of the property’s value, known as the loan to value. Interest is often ‘rolled up’ and paid at the end rather than monthly. The loan is repaid in one go when you sell the property or move onto longer-term finance.

The exit strategy

Lenders care most about how you’ll repay, which is known as the exit. It’s usually a sale, a refinance onto a commercial or buy-to-let mortgage, or funds arriving from elsewhere. A clear, realistic exit makes everything else easier.

Common uses

  • Buying commercial premises or a unit before a longer-term mortgage is ready.
  • Auction purchases with tight completion deadlines.
  • Buying and refurbishing a property that isn’t mortgageable yet.
  • Releasing equity from a property quickly for a business opportunity.

A note on your home

Bridging secured on a home you or your family live in is regulated differently and needs a specialist. If that’s your situation, we’ll tell you and point you in the right direction.

Questions people ask

Why is bridging more expensive than a mortgage?

It’s short-term and fast to arrange, and lenders price it for that. Because terms are short, the total cost can still be reasonable if the exit happens on time.

What happens if my exit is delayed?

Costs build up, and some lenders charge default rates after the term ends. That’s why a realistic exit plan matters.

Can I bridge onto a commercial mortgage?

Yes, that’s a common route: bridge to buy or improve, then refinance once the property qualifies.
