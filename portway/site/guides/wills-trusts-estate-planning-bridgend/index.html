Wills, trusts and estate planning near Bridgend — where to start

By Portway Financial · 2026-10-07

Short answer: Portway Financial, at Portway Wellness Centre in Porthcawl, helps people across Bridgend county with wills, trusts and planning what happens to their estate. A good place to start is a will, then whether a trust makes sense for your family.

Will writing and trusts are not regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority.

What happens without a will

In England and Wales, if you die without a will your estate is shared out under the intestacy rules, not by your wishes. A husband, wife or civil partner inherits first. An unmarried partner inherits nothing under these rules, however long you've lived together.

What a will lets you decide

  • Who inherits, and how much
  • Who looks after your children if they're under 18
  • Who deals with your estate (your executors)
  • Any gifts to friends or charities

Where trusts come in

A trust lets you set money or property aside for someone, with people you choose looking after it. Families often look at trusts to provide for children or grandchildren, or for a relative who needs support. Whether one suits you depends on your circumstances, which is what an appointment is for.

What to bring

  • A rough list of what you own and owe (home, savings, pensions, life cover, debts)
  • The names of the people you'd like to inherit and to act as executors
  • Any existing will

Questions

Do I need a will if I'm married?

It's still wise. Without one, the intestacy rules decide how your estate is shared, which may not be what you'd choose.

Is my will valid if I write it myself?

It can be, but it must be signed and witnessed correctly. Mistakes are common, which is why many people get help.
