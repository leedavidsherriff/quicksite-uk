Cookies

Last updated 24 September 2026

The short version

We use one optional thing that sets cookies: the Meta pixel, which tells us whether our Facebook and Instagram adverts work. It only loads if you press Accept on our cookie banner. Press No thanks and it never loads, and the site works exactly the same.

What a cookie is

A cookie is a small file a website stores on your device, usually so it can recognise you again later. Similar tools, like a tracking pixel or local storage, work in much the same way, and the law treats them the same, so this page covers all of them.

The Meta pixel, in plain English

The Meta pixel is a small piece of code from Meta Platforms Ireland Limited, the company behind Facebook and Instagram. If you press Accept, it loads and tells Meta that this device visited a page of this site, along with technical details your browser sends to any website: the page address, your IP address, and your browser and device type. If you're logged in to Facebook or Instagram on the same browser, Meta can link the visit to your account.

We use it for one purpose: measuring our adverts. We see totals, like how many people visited after seeing a particular advert. We never see who you are, and we can't look up any individual's browsing.

Meta also uses this information under its own terms, including to improve its advertising systems. Meta is responsible for that use, and its privacy policy explains it. The data may be processed outside the UK, including in the United States, under the safeguards Meta describes there.

Before you press Accept, none of this happens. The pixel isn't just switched off: this site doesn't even request it from Meta's servers.

Changing your mind

You can change your choice whenever you like, on any page. Use the Cookie settings link at the bottom of the page to bring the banner back. If you switch from Accept to No thanks, we delete the Meta cookies this site set and reload the page without the pixel. You can also clear cookies in your browser settings at any time, and we'll ask again on your next visit.

Page counts (no cookie)

Separately, we count page views so we know which pages get read. This doesn't use a cookie and doesn't need your consent. When a page loads it sends us two things: the name of the page, and the domain of the site that sent you here (for example facebook.com). We don't collect your IP address, your device, or any ID that could be matched to you later. It runs whatever you choose on the banner.
