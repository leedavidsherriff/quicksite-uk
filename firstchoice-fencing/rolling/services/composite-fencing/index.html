Composite fencing: fit it and forget it

Wood-plastic boards in aluminium posts. Never needs painting and will not rot.

Composite fencing is made from boards of recycled wood fibre and plastic that slot, one on top of the next, between aluminium posts. It looks like a modern horizontal board fence but it does not rot, split, warp or need painting, ever. You pay more for it on the day, often around twice the price of a good timber fence, and in return you get a fence that looks the same in ten years as it does the week it goes up, with nothing more than a wash now and then. For people who never want to stain a fence again, or for gardens where the fence is hard to reach, it is worth a serious look.

How composite systems work

Aluminium posts are set in concrete, or bolted to a base plate on an existing patio or wall, at the spacing the system is designed for, usually around 1.8 metres. The composite boards are tongue-and-groove, so each one locks into the one below, and they slide down into channels in the posts. A top rail caps the run and a bottom rail keeps the lowest board off the ground. Because the posts and boards are made to fit each other, the lines are very straight, but the posts must be set precisely, as there is almost no room to fudge a spacing.

What it is good at, and what it is not

Composite does not rot, cannot be eaten by insects, does not splinter and keeps its colour well, particularly the capped boards with a protective outer shell. It is heavy and stiff, and with proper footings it handles wind well. Its weaknesses are cost and repair: if a board gets damaged you usually have to lift the boards above it out to change it, and colours are made in batches, so a replacement years later may not match exactly. Dark colours also get warm in direct sun. We will tell you honestly whether it makes sense for your garden.

Colours and mixing materials

Most systems come in greys, charcoals and warm timber tones, with black or anthracite aluminium posts. A popular approach is to use composite where the fence is most visible, around a patio or seating area, and a matching timber style for long side boundaries where cost matters more. We can also combine composite boards with a timber or aluminium gate so the whole boundary reads as one.

Upkeep

A wash with warm soapy water and a soft brush once or twice a year is all it needs. Avoid pressure-washing at close range, which can mark the surface. There is nothing to paint or treat, the aluminium posts will not rust, and there are no nails to work loose. Keep soil and bark off the bottom rail so water can drain away freely.

How long it takes

Typically 2 to 3 days for 20 metres, weather allowing.

Questions

Composite fencing: what people ask.

Is composite fencing worth the money?

If you never want to paint a fence again and plan to stay put, it often is. It costs more to fit than timber, around £170 to £195 per metre as a guide at 1.8 m, but it needs no treatment and will not rot, so the running cost is close to nothing.

How long does composite fencing last?

Good quality systems are commonly expected to last 20 to 25 years or more, and the aluminium posts will not rot at ground level, which is where timber fences usually fail.

Can composite fencing be fitted to an existing wall or patio?

Often, yes. The aluminium posts can be bolted to a base plate on a sound concrete base or wall rather than concreted into the ground. We check the base first, because the posts are only as strong as what they are fixed to.
