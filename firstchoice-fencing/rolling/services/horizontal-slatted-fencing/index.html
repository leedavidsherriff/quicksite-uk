Horizontal slatted fencing that runs dead straight

Modern horizontal boards with even gaps and hidden fixings: the look that changes a garden.

Horizontal slatted fencing is the style that makes a plain back garden look designed. Long boards run across between the posts, usually with a small, even gap between them, so light comes through while the view is broken up. It suits new-build and modern homes, and it lifts an ordinary semi or ex-council garden more than almost anything else you can do to the boundary. It is also the style that shows sloppy work fastest: a gap that wanders, a board that sags between posts or a line that steps up and down with the ground. Every board has to be level and every gap the same, so this is careful work.

Slats, gaps and privacy

The usual build uses planed boards around 70 to 95 mm deep with gaps of 5 to 20 mm. Narrow gaps give near-total privacy and a clean, solid look; wider gaps let more light and wind through and feel lighter. If privacy matters most, a board-on-board version puts slats on both sides of the posts, offset, so you cannot see straight through at all. We fix boards with stainless or coated screws, which will not leave black streaks in the timber, and we space them with a fixed spacer rather than by eye so the gaps stay identical from one end of the garden to the other.

Posts and spans in a windy garden

Horizontal boards bend if they span too far, so posts go closer together than with panels, typically every 1.5 to 1.8 metres, with an intermediate batten where needed to stop the boards bowing. Because slatted fencing lets some wind through it copes better with a gale than a solid wall of boards, but the posts still do the work, so they go around 600 mm into concrete. We can finish the posts in timber to match, or use dark grey aluminium or powder-coated steel posts, which frame the boards neatly and never rot.

Which timber

Pressure-treated softwood is the budget choice and takes a stain well; it is often supplied pre-coloured. Scottish larch is a naturally durable homegrown timber with a warm colour that silvers attractively if left untreated. Western red cedar is lighter, very stable, naturally rot-resistant and smells wonderful, but it costs more. Whatever the timber, it will move with the seasons, which is why the fixing and spacing matter. We will show you samples and tell you honestly how each will look after three winters, not just on the day it goes up.

On a sloping garden

Horizontal fencing does not follow a slope the way vertical boards can. On a sloping garden we step the fence, keeping every bay level and dropping the height at each post, or we build a low retaining plinth so the fence sits on a level line. Stepping looks crisp when the steps are even, so we set out the whole run before the first post goes in. Talk to us about levels at the survey; it affects both the look and the price.

Typically 2 to 3 days for 20 to 25 metres, weather allowing.

Questions

Horizontal slatted fencing: what people ask.

How much does horizontal slatted fencing cost?

As a guide, a 1.8 m slatted fence in treated softwood runs about £105 to £120 per metre fitted, and larch or cedar about £150 to £170. The main things that move the price are the timber, how close the gaps are, post material and whether the garden slopes.

Is slatted fencing private?

With narrow gaps of 5 to 10 mm it is very private from most angles, and a board-on-board version is fully private. With wider gaps you can see through when you stand close, which some people prefer because it keeps the garden light.

Will horizontal boards sag?

Not if the posts are close enough together and the boards are the right thickness for the span. Sagging slatted fences almost always have posts too far apart, so we keep spans short and add a centre batten where a board needs it.

Can I paint or stain slatted fencing?

Yes. Treated softwood takes a water-based stain well, and a dark colour such as charcoal or black is very popular with modern gardens. Larch and cedar can be oiled to hold their colour or left to weather to silver.
